\title{\texorpdfstring{\lambdap}{lambda-p}: Paths through Dependent Pairs}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We present \lambdap{}, a calculus in which paths traverse immutable dependent
pairs. A pair member may depend on the first component and may contain either
a term reference or a type definition. The calculus distinguishes term
singleton types from abstract type selections. Conflating the two makes
singleton symmetry unsound: we give a closed, well-typed program that becomes
stuck. We adopt a conservative rule for dependent-pair subtyping and prove
type safety in Lean. The unrestricted pair rule remains an open question.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{path-dependent types, dependent pairs, singleton types, type
soundness, Lean}

\maketitle

\section{Introduction}

A path-dependent type assigns a type component to a stable term path. The
usual object-calculus presentation combines this mechanism with recursive
objects, refinements, intersections, and a substantial subtyping relation
\citep{amin2014foundations,rompf2016dot}. \lambdap{} isolates paths, abstract
type members, and dependency between the two components of a pair.

\lambdap{} is a call-by-value calculus in monadic normal form whose values are
functions and immutable dependent pairs. Paths are formed from variables,
first projections, and labelled selections. A pair stores a first component
together with either a term reference or a type definition, and its member
signature may depend on the first component. Chains of pairs encode records:
lookup resumes through the first component when the outer label differs. Term
singleton types record path identity, while abstract selections range over
explicitly checked lower and upper bounds. The static semantics includes
dependent functions and declarative subtyping with primitive transitivity;
evaluation combines big-step path resolution with a CK machine over an
append-only store.

For example, let \(r_1=\pairval{b}{a}{z}\) and
\(r_2=\pairval{r_1}{A}{T}\). Selection \(r_2.A\) hits the outer type member,
whereas \(r_2.a\) misses \(A\) and continues as \(r_2.1.a\), eventually
returning \(z\). The signature of the outer member may refer to its binder for
\(r_1\), so the same pair structure supports both record extension and
dependency on the preceding component.

Soundness depends on two design choices. First, the singleton type
\(\sing{p}\), which expresses value identity, is distinct from the abstract
selection \(p.A\), which denotes a type between declared bounds. An earlier
version represented both with one constructor. We show that singleton
symmetry then turns abstract membership into a false path equality and yields
a closed stuck program.

Second, dependent-pair subtyping is conservative. The first-component type
may be widened separately; the dependent member may change only while the
first component is a singleton, with an explicit comparison after opening at
that path. This rule supports the intended abstraction examples and supplies
the evidence used by a direct semantic proof. Soundness of the unrestricted
rule with distinct singleton and selection types remains open.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
The paper gives a closed counterexample showing that abstract membership
cannot justify singleton symmetry; a calculus that separates those notions
and retains dependent abstract members with explicit bound checks; and a
direct type-safety proof based on exact stores, co-resolution of paths,
and a finite semantic interpretation of subtyping. The complete development
is formalized in Lean. Recursive objects, recursive types, mutation,
intersections, and unions lie outside the core language; termination, type
inference, and elimination of primitive transitivity lie outside the present
development.

The binder \(x\) in a function scopes over its codomain. In
\(\pairty{x}{S}{a}{\tau}\), it scopes over the member signature \(\tau\).

generalized-type well-formedness, and term typing. Their judgment forms are
displayed with the rules below. The double colon in precise path typing marks

through \textsc{T-Sub}.

\subsection{Dynamics}
\label{sec:dynamics}

A store \(\sigma\) is an immutable sequence of values, and a stored value may
refer only to earlier cells. We write \(\sigma(x)=v\) for lookup and
\(\sigma,x\mapsto v\) for extension at a fresh location.

Source path resolution, written \(\sigma\vdash p\resolve x\), is big-step.
Projection returns the stored first component, a matching term member returns
its stored location, and a missed label continues from the first component.
Resolution is deterministic but partial on arbitrary stores; totality for
well-typed paths is part of the soundness argument.

Machine states are triples \(\langle\sigma,K,t\rangle\), with continuations
made only of let frames. The rules at the bottom of \cref{fig:dynamics}
resolve applications and paths, evaluate let-bound computations, and
allocate syntactic values. Store extension weakens the old continuation,
written \(K^\uparrow\), into the extended scope. Application first resolves
both operand paths; the canonical-forms lemma establishes the abstraction
shape required at the operator location.

\section{Two design decisions}
\label{sec:pairs}

\subsection{Singletons and abstract selections}

The term singleton \(\sing{p}\) asserts value identity. In particular,
\textsc{S-Alias} is justified when precise typing says that one path has
another path's singleton type. An abstract selection \(p.A\) denotes an
unknown type between the bounds stored at \(A\); membership in \(p.A\)
carries no path-equality information.

In a precursor to \lambdap{}, both forms used one type constructor, so
singleton symmetry accepted either role. Let
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
f &=& \lambda(x:\top).x,\\
q &=& \pairval{f}{A}{\top},\\
h &=& \lambda(z:q.A).q.
\end{array}
\]
The historical rules type the body of \(h\) by the chain
\[
  \sing{q}<:\top<:q.A<:\sing{z}.
\]
The last edge applies singleton symmetry to the context binding \(z:q.A\),
treating abstract membership as equality. The same rules accept \(f\) as an
argument of type \(q.A\). The application \(h\,f\) therefore evaluates to
\(q\) despite receiving the result type \(\sing{f}\).

Consequently, the closed program
\[
\begin{array}{l}
\mathbf{let}\ f=\lambda(x:\top).x\ \mathbf{in}\\
\mathbf{let}\ q=\pairval{f}{A}{\top}\ \mathbf{in}\\
\mathbf{let}\ h=\lambda(z:q.A).q\ \mathbf{in}\\
\mathbf{let}\ r=h\,f\ \mathbf{in}\ r\,f
\end{array}
\]
reaches an application whose operator location contains the pair \(q\). It is
neither final nor reducible. Every interval in the derivation is
\(\top..\top\), so all bounds pass the consistency check. An admissible
transitivity lemma would compose the same chain.

In the present syntax, a binding \(z:q.A\) cannot be a premise for
\textsc{S-Alias}; the historical derivation and execution are machine checked
separately.

The counterexample shows that singleton symmetry must distinguish term
identity from abstract membership. Separate constructors make this
distinction explicit. A suitably guarded symmetry rule would provide another
possible repair.

\subsection{The pair rule}

The unrestricted rule one might first write is
\[
\inferrule
  {\Gamma\vdash S<:S'
   \\
   \Gamma,x:S\vdash\tau<:\tau'}
  {\Gamma\vdash
    \pairty{x}{S}{a}{\tau}
    <:
    \pairty{x}{S'}{a}{\tau'}}.
\tag{Unrestricted Pair}
\]
The soundness proof interprets each subtyping derivation by a finite witness,
called a \emph{semantic coercion}, that maps realizations of its source type
to realizations of its target type. \Cref{sec:realization} gives the formal
definition.
Its semantic interpretation must work for the actual run-time first
component \(y\). In particular, a coercion between the pair types must later
turn a realization of \(\subst{\tau}{y}{x}\) into one of
\(\subst{\tau'}{y}{x}\). The induction hypothesis supplies this evidence only
after \(y\) and its realization have been given. The pair rule must construct
its finite witness earlier, before evaluation reveals \(y\). Storing an
inhabitant-indexed function in the coercion would make the realization
relation occur negatively in its own mutual definition.

\textsc{S-Pair-Single} fixes a path \(p\) and supplies that specialized
comparison explicitly. If the stored first component \(y\) has type
\(\sing{p}\), then \(y\) and \(p\) co-resolve. Run-time conversion transports
the opened comparison at \(p\) to the member opened at \(y\).

Together, the two pair rules support the intended abstraction pattern. If
\(\Gamma\vdash p::P\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\pairty{x}{\sing{p}}{A}{\sing{p}..\sing{p}}
\\[-1mm]
&\quad<:\pairty{x}{\sing{p}}{A}{\bot..\sing{x}}
\\[-1mm]
&\quad<:\pairty{x}{P}{A}{\bot..\sing{x}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first step hides an exact definition behind dependent bounds while the
first component is a singleton; the second widens that component.

The restriction arises from the current semantic proof. Soundness of the
unrestricted rule remains open; we have neither a proof nor a counterexample
for \lambdap{}.

\section{Soundness}
\label{sec:soundness}

The proof records store locations at their exact introduction types, relates
paths that resolve to the same location, and uses semantic coercions to
interpret subtyping. The operational semantics is the machine in
\cref{sec:dynamics}.

Write \(\initial(t)\steps s\) when the initial machine state of \(t\) reaches
\(s\) in finitely many steps. The source-facing result is the usual
non-stuckness theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Type safety]
\label{thm:safety}
If \(\ctxempty\vdash t:T\) and \(\initial(t)\steps s\), then
\(s\) is final or there exists \(s'\) such that \(s\sstep s'\).
\end{theorem}

The theorem concerns finite execution prefixes; termination lies outside its
scope. Its proof maintains a stronger run-time invariant over exact stores and
machine states.

Write \(\Delta\struct_{\exact}s:U\) when the store of \(s\) is recorded at
exact introduction types and its current term and continuation satisfy the
structural run-time judgments. Write
\((\Gamma,T)\preceq(\Delta,U)\) when \(\Delta\) extends \(\Gamma\) by
allocations and \(U\) is the corresponding weakening of \(T\).

\begin{theorem}[Reachable-state invariant]
\label{thm:preservation}
If \(\ctxempty\vdash t:T\) and \(\initial(t)\steps s\), then for some
\(\Delta,U\),
\[
(\ctxempty,T)\preceq(\Delta,U)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Delta\struct_{\exact}s:U.
\]
\end{theorem}

The changing context is bookkeeping forced by allocation: a fresh location
extends the store context, and the result type is weakened along that
extension. The exact structural judgment is the mathematical preservation
invariant; \cref{thm:safety} is its source-facing corollary.

The proof proceeds through the relations summarized in
\cref{fig:runtime-proof,fig:realization}. An exact store retains the
syntax-directed type of every allocated value. Generalized resolution then
follows a well-typed path to a value location or a stored type definition,
and co-resolution accounts for the path equalities created by lookup. The
source rules embed into structural judgments parameterized by co-resolution.
A simultaneous induction interprets structural path typing by endpoint
realization and structural subtyping by semantic coercions. Coercion action
gives canonical forms; progress and preservation then follow by induction on
the machine state and transition, respectively.

\subsection{Path resolution and exact stores}

Lookup preserves structural checking, while precise synthesized types can
change.
Suppose a stored pair has first component \(y\). The path \(p.1\) may
synthesize \(\sing{y}\), whereas the endpoint \(y\) synthesizes the type
assigned by the store context. Ordinary typing relates the two without
requiring syntactic equality. Accordingly, the lookup argument separates
totality from endpoint checking. For the term-kind fragment of structural
path checking, an exact store gives
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\Gamma\struct_{\sigma}p::T
\qquad
\storeexact(\Gamma;\sigma)
\\ \hline
\exists x.\ \sigma\vdash p\resolve\mathsf{val}(x)
\ \land\ \Gamma\struct_{\sigma}x::T.
\end{array}
\]
The induction follows the same hit and miss structure as \textsc{P-Sel} and
\textsc{P-Miss}; structural subsumption absorbs the change from the path's
precise type to the context type of its endpoint.

Exactness is essential for the next step. An ordinary store typing may record
a location at any supertype of its stored value, hiding the information needed
by both lookup and canonical forms. The lookup lemmas above are therefore
stated directly for exact stores. From a location viewed as a function one
must recover an abstraction and its original signature. An exact store records
the syntax-directed type assigned by
\textsc{T-Abs}, \textsc{T-Pair}, or \textsc{T-Type-Pair}. Allocation extends
the store with precisely this type; value inversion and narrowing recheck the
suspended let body under the more informative binding.

\subsection{Co-resolution and structural typing}

Reduction also changes the spelling of paths. Let \(p\runtimeeq q\) be the
least congruence containing paths that resolve to the same location in
\(\sigma\). Types admit conversion along this equivalence. For example, a
selected member opened at \(p.1\) must be related to the same member opened at
the location returned by the projection. Co-resolution supplies exactly that
equality.

The source judgments embed into proof-only structural judgments for paths,
subtyping, terms, continuations, and states. These judgments expose the source
constructors together with conversion by \(\runtimeeq\) and serve as the
run-time preservation invariant. For a path congruence \(R\), write
\(\Gamma\struct_R J\) for the auxiliary judgment generated by the source
rules and conversion along \(R\); \(\Gamma\struct_{\sigma}J\) abbreviates the
instance \(R=\runtimeeq\). A representative rule replaces co-resolving paths
inside a type:
\[
\inferrule
  {p\runtimeeq q}
  {\Gamma\struct_{\sigma}
    \subst{\tau}{p}{x}<:\subst{\tau}{q}{x}}.
\tag{Conversion}
\]
Because \(\runtimeeq\) is a congruence, the same principle applies beneath
projections, selections, functions, and pair signatures.

\subsection{Realization and semantic coercions}
\label{sec:realization}

Generalized resolution in \cref{fig:runtime-proof} follows both term and type
members. A value endpoint realizes a proper type, while a type-definition
endpoint realizes an interval. The selected clauses in
\cref{fig:realization} retain the information later consumed by canonical
forms. Function realization records the stored abstraction, its exact closure
signature, and the domain and codomain comparisons from that signature to the
observed function type. Pair realization records realizations of the first
component and of the member opened at that component. Singleton realization
is resolution to the designated location; selection realization first
resolves the type member to its stored definition.

Interval realization is the recursive point of the interpretation. If a
stored definition \(W\) realizes \(L..U\), the proof needs operations taking
realizers of \(L\) to realizers of \(W\), and realizers of \(W\) to realizers
of \(U\). Placing those operations directly in the definition as functions
would put the realization relation to the left of an arrow in its own mutual
definition. The metatheory defunctionalizes them into finite, proof-relevant
codes, written
\(\Gamma;\sigma\vdash\tau_1\semmap\tau_2\), and called semantic coercions.
They occur only in the metatheory. The operational semantics remains the CK
machine of \cref{fig:dynamics}.

Each coercion constructor retains the structural premises of the subtyping
rule it interprets. Erasure recovers a structural subtyping derivation, while
the action theorem maps any endpoint realization of the source to one of the
target. Reflexivity and transitivity become identity and composition.
Selection resolves the member definition and uses the lower or upper code
stored by interval realization. A function code preserves the residual
domain and codomain comparisons needed when the abstraction is applied.
First-component pair widening acts recursively on the stored component.
Interval subtyping composes its lower and upper maps in the variance-directed
order.

The dependent-member case accounts for the specialized source rule. A pair
whose first component \(y\) realizes \(\sing{p}\) supplies
\(y\runtimeeq p\). Conversion first changes the realized member from
\(\subst{\tau}{y}{x}\) to \(\subst{\tau}{p}{x}\); the explicit opened
coercion from \textsc{S-Pair-Single} acts at \(p\); a second conversion changes
the result to \(\subst{\tau'}{y}{x}\). The pair coercion stores the opened map
and the structural premises; its action performs the two conversions around
that map.

\subsection{Fundamental lemma and canonical forms}

The central induction is a simultaneous substitution argument. The realized
substitution judgment at the bottom of \cref{fig:realization} packages three
facts: each context binding maps to a structurally checked path at its
substituted type, path relations are preserved, and the mapped path resolves
to an endpoint realizing that type. This stronger statement supplies a
semantic endpoint whenever the induction enters a binder.

\begin{lemma}[Fundamental lemma]
\label{lem:fundamental}
Suppose \(\rho\models^R_{\Delta,\sigma}\Gamma\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(\Gamma\struct_R p::\tau\), then for some \(e\),
  \(\sigma\vdash\rho(p)\resolve e\) and
  \(e\in\llbracket\rho(\tau)\rrbracket_{\Delta,\sigma}\).
\item If \(\Gamma\struct_R\tau_1<:\tau_2\), then
  \(\Delta;\sigma\vdash
    \rho(\tau_1)\semmap\rho(\tau_2)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Once a realized substitution is supplied, the simultaneous induction does
not require a separate exactness premise. Exact stores establish the identity
instance \(\mathsf{id}\models^{\runtimeeq}_{\Gamma,\sigma}\Gamma\), which is
the instance used by the run-time proof.

The cases for selection use interval realization; transitivity composes
coercions. In the singleton-pair case, the first component co-resolves with
the fixed path \(p\), so run-time conversion transports the explicit opened
coercion to the actual component. Instantiating \cref{lem:fundamental} with
the identity substitution gives generalized path resolution and a semantic
coercion for every structural subtyping derivation in an exact store.

Applying such a coercion to the realization of a location singleton yields
the canonical forms used by the machine.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical forms]
\label{lem:canonical}
Let \(\sigma\) be exact for \(\Gamma\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(\Gamma\struct_{\sigma}\sing{x}<:(z:S)\to T\), then
  \(\sigma(x)\) is an
  abstraction.
\item If \(\Gamma\struct_{\sigma}\sing{x}<:\pairty{z}{S}{a}{\tau}\), then
  \(\sigma(x)\) is a pair.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

The function case also recovers the exact closure signature and the residual
subtypings relating it to \((z:S)\to T\). Those residuals are what make
dependent beta reduction type preserving.

\subsection{Progress and preservation}

Progress is by induction on structural typing of the current term and
continuation. In an application, path resolution supplies locations for the
operator and operand, and \cref{lem:canonical} exposes an abstraction at the
operator. Projection and selection use the pair part of canonical forms and
the hit/miss correspondence between static and dynamic lookup.

Preservation is by cases on the machine step. If \(p\) normalizes to \(x\),
co-resolution converts \(\sing{p}\) to \(\sing{x}\) inside the run-time
invariant, preserving the enclosing structural term judgment. Beta reduction
uses the closure signature and residual comparisons obtained from function
realization after coercion action.
When a let-bound value is allocated, exact value inversion determines the new
store binding, narrowing checks the suspended body at that exact type, and
the old continuation is weakened into the extended scope. The remaining
administrative steps preserve the invariant directly. Iterating preservation
and applying progress at the endpoint proves \cref{thm:safety}.

The calculus, counterexample, and safety proof are mechanized in Lean 4
\citep{demoura2021lean4}.

\section{Related work}

DOT combines abstract type members with recursive objects, refinements,
intersections, and a rich subtyping lattice
\citep{amin2014foundations,rompf2016dot}. Its soundness proofs must control
which abstract bounds can be exposed in arbitrary contexts. The tight-typing
account of \citet{rapoport2017simple}, for example, factors reasoning through
inert contexts in which run-time values admit strong inversion principles.

For \lambdap{}, the exact-store invariant supplies the required inversion
principle directly: a location retains the introduction type of its stored
function or pair even when declarative subtyping presents it at another type.
Semantic coercions interpret primitive transitivity and abstract selection
without a separate inert/tight-typing judgment.

pDOT adds arbitrary-length paths and singleton types to DOT and gives a
Coq-mechanized safety proof \citep{rapoport2019pdot}. It is the closest point
of comparison for path equality and abstract selection. \lambdap{} represents
record extension by dependent pair chains, with explicit missed-label rules
for both static selection and dynamic lookup. The resulting proof isolates
the obligations induced by path lookup once recursive objects, intersections,
and unions are absent.

\section{Conclusion}

\lambdap{} isolates paths, dependent pairs, and abstract type members. Its
counterexample shows that abstract membership cannot justify singleton
symmetry. Separating the two notions and conservatively restricting pair
subtyping yields a direct type-safety proof. Whether unrestricted pair
subtyping is sound for \lambdap{} remains open.
