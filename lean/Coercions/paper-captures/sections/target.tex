\section{The Full \treeK Target}\label{sec:target}
% unit macros
\providecommand{\yields}{\mathrel{\Longrightarrow}}
\mathtgt

This section gives the whole target of \treeK. It adds to the fragment of Section~\ref{sec:mini} what \Rone adds to its own, now with capture sets, and three things more: capture names with capture members, a store in the theorem about capture evidence, and boxes. Figures~\ref{fig:target-syntax} to~\ref{fig:target-capev} give the calculus where it differs from report~1's Figures~\ref{R1-fig:target-syntax} to~\ref{R1-fig:machine}. Its theorems are proven in \treeK, and Table~\ref{tab:matrix} lists those that another tree lacks or states differently.

\paragraph{A program with a capability member.}
The reactor of Section~\ref{sec:cc-later} lets a handler of a \lstinline{GUIReactor} use \lstinline{ui} and \lstinline{log} and nothing else. The Lean's example C2 has this shape over two platform capabilities $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$. Its object type, the translation of a DOT type that Section~\ref{sec:source} writes out, is
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\mathit{Obj} = \mu(\self.\,[\,\plec{\cempty}{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}},\ \plec{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}}{\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}},\ \phas{\mathit{run}}, \\
\qquad\qquad \ple{\sel{\self}{\mathit{run}}}{\pit[w]{\Unit}{\Unit}},\ \plec{\cset{\sel{\self}{\mathit{run}}}}{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}}\,]) .
\end{array}
\]
The promise is that a call of $\mathit{run}$ is charged to the member's bound $\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}$. Section~\ref{sec:target-members} gives the evidence for that charge, and Section~\ref{sec:target-canon} the theorem that makes it mean something.

\subsection{What the full target adds}\label{sec:target-delta}

A type is a shape with a capture set, $\capt{S}{C}$, and report~1's types are the shapes, with the box $\boxt{T}$ of Section~\ref{sec:target-boxes} added. Report~1's inclusion evidence becomes shape inclusion $e$, and a type inclusion $\ev{capt}\,e\,f$ pairs it with capture inclusion $f$. The function shape takes type inclusions for its domain and codomain (\rn{Sh-Pi}), so a cast checks the capture sets on both sides of an arrow. Casts supply the subsumption that the fragment did without. A capture set may contain a capture name $\sel{x}{\ell}$, the capture set named $\ell$ in the block of $x$, and telescopes gain capture inclusions and equalities.

Atoms gain $\ev{recap}\,a\,f$, which keeps the shape of $a$ and takes any capture set that $f$ puts above $\cset{\rt{a}}$ (\rn{A-Recap}). With $f = \ev{refl}\,\cset{x}$ it types $x$ at $\capt{S}{\cset{x}}$, the variable rule of capture calculi (Section~\ref{sec:source}). Every wrapper keeps the root, so \rn{C-Var} and \rn{C-Member} read the same variable however an atom is wrapped (\lean{C4\_capvar}, \lean{C4\_member}, decided). A projection has the block name of the field at its capture name (\rn{T-Proj}). A literal carries an annotation $A$ and capture witnesses $W^{c}$, and each of its fields carries closing evidence against $A$ and the self (\rn{F-Cons}). Figure~\ref{fig:target-rules} gives the rules that are new or changed. Its steps read two head forms that report~1's Figure~\ref{R1-fig:forms} lacks: $\form{eqv}\,\varphi$ for closed equality evidence, which report~1 folds into $\form{id}$, and $\form{boxed}\,d$ for an inclusion of boxes (Section~\ref{sec:mech-trees}).

A context gives each capture binder a bound $B$. No typing rule adds a capture binder to a context and no step allocates one, so no term abstracts over a capture set, and DOT quantifies over capture sets through members.

\subsection{Capture names and capture members}\label{sec:target-members}

A source capture member becomes two capture inclusions on the capture name of the self, as in $\mathit{Obj}$. A field gets a third proposition after report~1's two, its declared capture set. Figure~\ref{fig:source-trans} gives both clauses. A literal defines its capture names by its capture witnesses. Its precise telescope $\ofLit{W, W^{c}}{\vec{\ell}}$ lists one equality $\peq{\sel{\self}{\ell}}{W(\ell)}$ per witness, then one capture equality $\peqc{\cset{\sel{\self}{\ell}}}{W^{c}(\ell)}$ per capture witness, then one presence per field. A store binder records the capture witnesses, and $\Gamma(x) \ni \ell \mathrel{:=_{c}} C$ says that the witness of $x$ at $\ell$ is $C$, with $C = \cempty$ for an unlisted $\ell$.

Figure~\ref{fig:target-capev} gives capture evidence in full. \rn{C-Member} eliminates a capture inclusion at an atom, as report~1's $\ev{member}$ eliminates an inclusion, and \rn{CE-Def} reads a capture name of a transparent binder. The capture template $\ev{leC}\,p\,H\,p'$ proves a target capture inclusion from the source capture proposition that its hole names. Its sides chain closed capture evidence, weakened under the self, and syntactic inclusions $\ev{incl}\,X\,Y$, which may mention the self.

C2's client shows the charge (\lean{C2\_client}, decided). In the context $\kappa_1 : \cstar, \kappa_2 : \cstar, x : \capt{\mathit{Obj}}{\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}}, u : \Unit$ it reads $\mathit{run}$ off $x$ and calls it:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\klet r = \ev{cast}\,(\ev{proj}\,x\,\mathit{run}\,h_2)\,(\ev{capt}\,e_3\,f_4) \kin r\,u \\
\qquad \ann{\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2};\ \ev{union}\,(\ev{trans}\,(\ev{capvar}\,r)\,f_1)\,(\ev{trans}\,(\ev{capvar}\,u)\,(\ev{elem}\,\cempty\,\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}))} .
\end{array}
\]
Here $r$ has the type $\capt{(\pit[w]{\Unit}{\Unit})}{\cset{\sel{x}{C}}}$, since $h_2$, $e_3$, $f_4$ and $f_1$ are $\ev{member}\,x\,(\ev{refl}\,\mathit{Obj})\,i$ at $i = 2, 3, 4, 1$. The avoidance evidence charges the call. \rn{C-Var} puts $\cset{r}$ below $\cset{\sel{x}{C}}$, and $f_1$ reads the member's upper bound at $x$, $\cset{\sel{x}{C}} \le \cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}$. The client is checked once, for every object of type $\mathit{Obj}$.

\subsection{Leaves with names}\label{sec:target-leaves}

The leaves at the end of Figure~\ref{fig:target-capev} extend those of Figure~\ref{fig:mini-syntax} in two ways. First, a capture name follows the capture witness of its binder and spends one unit of fuel. Names can form a cycle, since a witness may name a label of its own block. A set has a leaf when some fuel reaches it, so a cycle contributes nothing, as report~1's cyclic alias resolves to $\top$, which asserts nothing.

Second, a capture binder bound $\cstar$ or $\scopeb$ is its own leaf, and one bound $\mathsf{upper}\,C$ or $\instb{C}$ has the leaves of $C$. The bounds $\scopeb$, $\mathsf{upper}\,C$ and $\instb{C}$ are the scope, bounded and instance binders of \treeCC (Section~\ref{sec:levels-scopes}), and they are in the syntax of all three trees. No clause returns a term variable or a capture name, so every leaf is a capture binder bound $\cstar$ or $\scopeb$. \treeK constructs only $\cstar$, for the platform, but store typing accepts any bound (\rn{S-Cap}), and the theorems below hold over stores with any bounds. A capture name of an opaque binder has no leaves, and over a store every term binder is transparent.

\subsection{Canonical forms of closed capture evidence}\label{sec:target-canon}

Report~1's Theorem~\ref{R1-thm:canon} assumes a typed store, $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$, and normalizes closed evidence to a typed head form. The normalizer drops the capture half of $\ev{capt}\,e\,f$, and the canonical form of capture evidence is a fact about leaves, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:mini-cap}. That theorem held in every context. With capture members it cannot, since under a binder of type $\obj{[\,\plec{\cset{\kappa}}{\cempty}\,]}$ one $\ev{member}$ proves $\cset{\kappa} \le \cempty$, and $\kappa$ is a leaf of $\cset{\kappa}$ only (Example~\ref{ex:target-bad}). Over a typed store it holds again.

The member cases read a view of an atom, which has one proposition form per proposition of its type (report~1's Figure~\ref{R1-fig:forms}). At a capture proposition the form carries no data, and its typedness is the proposition at the root, read as a fact about leaves. We call such a form a slot. A slot gets its fact from the store. A stored literal has one slot per capture witness, with the fact of \rn{CE-Def}, and a capture template composes its sides with the source slot.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical forms of closed capture evidence, \leanin{cap\_canon}{\treeK}]\label{thm:target-canon}
Let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\clej{\Gamma}{f}{C}{D}$ then $\caple{\Gamma}{C}{D}$.
\item If $\ceqj{\Gamma}{\psi}{C}{D}$ then $\capeqv{\Gamma}{C}{D}$ (\lean{capeq\_canon}).
\item If $\atomj{\Gamma}{a}{\capt{S}{C}}$ then $\caple{\Gamma}{\cset{\rt{a}}}{C}$ (the capture conjunct of \lean{atom\_canon}).
\item If $\lej{\Gamma}{d}{\capt{S}{C}}{\capt{S'}{C'}}$ then $\caple{\Gamma}{C}{C'}$ (\lean{le\_canon\_cap}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By one mutual induction on typing derivations, together with report~1's Theorem~\ref{R1-thm:canon}, which the same induction proves at shapes (\lean{shape\_canon} and the view conjunct of \lean{atom\_canon}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \rn{C-Refl}, \rn{C-Trans}, \rn{C-Elem} and \rn{C-Union} as in Theorem~\ref{thm:mini-cap}. \rn{C-Var} is item 3, and \rn{C-EqToLe} is item 2.
\item \rn{C-Member} and \rn{CE-Member}. Report~1's theorem gives the view of $a$ and the form of $e$, and pushes the view through the form to a typed view of the target telescope. Its entry at $i$ is a slot, and its typedness is the conclusion, $\caple{\Gamma}{\inst{C_1}{\rt{a}}}{\inst{C_2}{\rt{a}}}$, or $\approx_\Gamma$ for an equality.
\item \rn{CE-Refl}, \rn{CE-Symm} and \rn{CE-Trans}: $\approx_\Gamma$ is an equivalence. \rn{CE-Def}: a defined capture name has the leaves of its witness, by the name clause (\lean{Ctx.Root\_name}).
\item Item 3. \rn{A-Var} is Lemma~\ref{lem:mini-var}, now at every fuel (\lean{Ctx.Root\_var}). \rn{A-Cast} is items 3 and 4, \rn{A-Fold}, \rn{A-Unfold} and \rn{A-Both} keep the capture set, and \rn{A-Recap} is item 1. Item 4. \rn{L-Capt} is item 1 at its capture half. \qedhere
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

Only the two member cases read the store, and \rn{CE-Def} needs a defined capture name but no store. \treeK states no store-free form of item 1 for evidence without $\ev{member}$ and $\ev{eqToLe}$. \treeCC does (Theorem~\ref{thm:root-inv}).

\begin{example}[A capture member through a literal, on paper]\label{ex:target-member}
Let the store hold $\kappa_1 : \cstar$, $\kappa_2 : \cstar$ and, at $y$, the literal $\litc{\cempty}{\tnil}{C = \cset{\kappa_1}}{\tnil}$, modelled on the object of C2 that defines $C$ as $\cset{\kappa_1}$. Its context $\Gamma$ types $y$ at $\capt{(\obj{\tel_y})}{\cempty}$ with $\tel_y = [\,\peqc{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}}{\cset{\kappa_1}}\,]$ and records $\Gamma(y) \ni C \mathrel{:=_{c}} \cset{\kappa_1}$. Let $\mathit{Abs} = \obj{[\,\plec{\cempty}{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}},\ \plec{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}}{\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}}\,]}$, the translation of the capture member of C2's object type, and
\[
m = [\,\ev{leC}\,(\ev{incl}\,\cempty\,\cset{\kappa_1} :: [\,])\,(\ev{eqSymC}\,0)\,[\,],\ \ev{leC}\,[\,]\,(\ev{eqC}\,0)\,(\ev{incl}\,\cset{\kappa_1}\,\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2} :: [\,])\,] .
\]
So $a = \ev{cast}\,y\,(\ev{capt}\,(\ev{obj}\,\tel_y\,m)\,(\ev{refl}\,\cempty))$ has type $\capt{\mathit{Abs}}{\cempty}$, and $\ev{member}\,a\,(\ev{refl}\,\mathit{Abs})\,1 : \cset{\sel{y}{C}} \le \cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}$ reads the upper bound at $y$.

In the member case of item 1, the view of $y$ has one slot. Its fact $\capeqv{\Gamma}{\cset{\sel{y}{C}}}{\cset{\kappa_1}}$ comes from the capture witness (\lean{Ctx.Root\_name}). The second template of $m$ turns it into slot~1 of the view of $a$. The fact of that slot composes the source slot read forwards with $\cset{\kappa_1} \subseteq \cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}$, and so $\caple{\Gamma}{\cset{\sel{y}{C}}}{\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}}$. Report~1's Example~\ref{R1-ex:mini-sel} makes the same computation and ends in the head form $\form{id}$. The nearest Lean statement, \lean{C5\_packing} in \treeK (decided), types such a cast for a literal with the capture witness $C = \cset{\mathit{fs}}$, and none computes the view.
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}[No closed escape of a star binder, \leanin{Store.Typed.no\_cap\_escape}{\treeK}]\label{cor:target-star}
Let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$ and $\Gamma(\kappa) = \cstar$. If $\kappa \notin \leaves{\Gamma}{D}$ then no $f$ has $\clej{\Gamma}{f}{\cset{\kappa}}{D}$. In particular no $f$ has $\clej{\Gamma}{f}{\cset{\kappa}}{\cempty}$ (\lean{Store.Typed.no\_cap\_star\_le\_nil}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\kappa$ is a leaf of $\cset{\kappa}$, so item 1 of Theorem~\ref{thm:target-canon} would make it a leaf of $D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Bad capture bounds, revisited]\label{ex:target-bad}
The target typing of C3, the closure of Section~\ref{sec:problem-bad}, is over one star binder $\kappa : \cstar$. Its outer binder has the object type $\mathit{Bad} = \obj{[\,\plec{\cset{\kappa}}{\cempty}\,]}$, whose one proposition states the bad inclusion directly:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\lambda^{\cempty}(x : \capt{\mathit{Bad}}{\cempty}).\,\lambda^{\cempty}(z : \capt{(\boxt{\capt{\top}{\cset{\kappa}}})}{\cempty}).\,\unboxc{z}{\cempty}{\ev{member}\,x\,(\ev{refl}\,\mathit{Bad})\,0} \\
\qquad \ann{\ev{elem}\,\cset{z}\,\cset{z}}\ \ann{\ev{elem}\,\cempty\,\cset{x}} .
\end{array}
\]
Its typing is decided (\lean{C3\_typed}), and so is $\ev{member}\,x\,(\ev{refl}\,\mathit{Bad})\,0 : \cset{\kappa} \le \cempty$ under both binders (\lean{C3\_badBounds}). The unboxing takes $\kappa$ off the boxed value and charges $\cempty$. Over a typed store no atom $a$ has a type $\capt{\mathit{Bad}}{D}$, since $\ev{member}\,a\,(\ev{refl}\,\mathit{Bad})\,0$ would be closed evidence that Corollary~\ref{cor:target-star} rules out. A call of the outer lambda from a typed state would hand its body such an atom, so the body never runs. The Lean states the corollary at such a binder as \lean{C3\_no\_store}, by hand, and the rest is report~1's argument for bad bounds on paper, with leaves for head forms.
\end{example}

\subsection{The use-set lemma and the rest of the metatheory}\label{sec:target-meta}

The use-set half of preservation keeps the shape of Lemma~\ref{lem:mini-step}. It gains a renaming $\rho$ of the de Bruijn binders of one store into the next, which is the identity with names.

\begin{lemma}[The use-set half of preservation, \leanin{step\_uses}{\treeK}]\label{lem:target-step}
Let $\statej{st}{T}$, let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$ for the store $\sigma$ of $st$, and let $st \step st'$, where $\sigma'$ is the store of $st'$. Then $\sextr{\rho}{\sigma}{\sigma'}$ for some renaming $\rho$, and $\caple{\Gamma'}{\uses{st'}}{\rho(\uses{st})}$ for every $\Gamma'$ with $\storej{\sigma'}{\Gamma'}$.
\end{lemma}

Table~\ref{tab:target-steps} gives the twelve cases. With the lemma, Theorems~\ref{thm:mini-pred} and~\ref{thm:mini-effect} hold for the full calculus with $\rho$ added, as \lean{capture\_prediction} and \lean{effect\_safety} in \treeK. Report~1's other theorems keep their form.

\begin{theorem}[Type safety and erasure, \lean{preservation'}, \lean{progress}, \lean{erase\_step} and \lean{erase\_reflect'} in \treeK]\label{thm:target-meta}

\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\statej{st}{T}$ and $st \step st'$, then $\statej{st'}{\rho(T)}$ for some renaming $\rho$.
\item If $\statej{st}{T}$, then $st$ is final or $st \step st'$ for some $st'$.
\item If $st \step st'$, then $st$ is a cast redex and $\erase{st} = \erase{st'}$, or $\erase{st} \step \erase{st'}$ in the runtime.

\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Boxes}\label{sec:target-boxes}

As Section~\ref{sec:cc-later} quoted, boxing hides a capture set and unboxing recovers it, and \treeK keeps both explicit. Boxing is a value, $\ev{box}\,a : \capt{(\boxt{T})}{\cempty}$ (\rn{V-Box}), so a container of capabilities can be pure, and a store location that holds a box has no leaves. Unboxing is a term, $\unboxc{a}{U}{f}$, with the charge $f : C \le U$ of the boxed set $C$ against the declared use set $U$ (\rn{T-Unbox}). The capture set $D$ of the box itself plays no part. \rn{E-UnboxRefl} hands back the stored atom when the chain of casts of $a$ normalizes to $\form{id}$ or $\form{eqv}\,\varphi$, and it fires at a bare variable too. \rn{E-UnboxCast} hands it back under $d$ when the chain normalizes to $\form{boxed}\,d$, as \rn{E-AppCast} hands a closure its argument under the domain coercion.

\begin{theorem}[Closed box inversion, \leanin{closed\_box\_inversion}{\treeK}]\label{thm:target-box}
Let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$ and $\atomj{\Gamma}{a}{\capt{(\boxt{T})}{D}}$. Then $\sigma(\rt{a}) = \ev{box}\,b$ for some atom $b$, and $\chn{\sigma}{a}{n}{F}$ for some $n$ and $F$ with $F = \form{id}$, or $F = \form{eqv}\,\varphi$ for some $\varphi$, or $F = \form{boxed}\,d$ for some $d$.
\end{theorem}

Progress and the backward erasure simulation use Theorem~\ref{thm:target-box}. The use-set lemma uses the typed chain.

C7 is a pure container of two boxed capabilities (\lean{C7\_client}, decided). Its object type is
\[
\mathit{Pair} = \obj{\tel_1 \mathbin{+\!\!+} \tel_2} \quad\text{with}\quad \tel_i = [\,\phas{e_i},\ \ple{\sel{\self}{e_i}}{\boxt{\CapT{\kappa_i}}},\ \plec{\cset{\sel{\self}{e_i}}}{\cempty}\,] .
\]
Both fields declare $\cempty$, so $o : \capt{\mathit{Pair}}{\cempty}$ is pure. The client binds $e_1$ of $o$ to $y : \capt{(\boxt{\CapT{\kappa_1}})}{\cempty}$ and opens it with $\unboxc{y}{\cset{\kappa_1}}{\ev{refl}\,\cset{\kappa_1}}$, in a let that declares $\cset{\kappa_1}$ and covers $\cset{y}$ by $\ev{capvar}\,y : \cset{y} \le \cempty$. With the declared sets $\cempty$ and the charge $\ev{elem}\,\cset{\kappa_1}\,\cempty$ the checker refuses the client (\lean{C7\_rejected}, decided).

Effect safety never flags an unboxing, since $\rt{a}$ holds a box and has no leaves. The next theorem speaks of the atom handed back.

\begin{theorem}[Effect safety at an unboxing, \leanin{effect\_safety\_unbox}{\treeK}]\label{thm:target-unbox}
Let $\statej{st}{T}$, let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$ for the store $\sigma$ of $st$, and let $\kappa \notin \leaves{\Gamma}{\uses{st}}$. Let $st \steps \mst{\sigma'}{K'}{\unboxc{a}{U}{f}}$ with $\sigma'(\rt{a}) = \ev{box}\,b$, and let $\storej{\sigma'}{\Gamma'}$. Then $\sextr{\rho}{\sigma}{\sigma'}$ and $\rho(\kappa) \notin \leaves{\Gamma'}{\cset{\rt{b}}}$ for some renaming $\rho$.
\end{theorem}

The proof takes the unboxing step that progress provides and applies capture prediction to the longer run. The theorem exists in \treeK only. A last theorem bounds an answer, not a run (\leanin{returned\_capture\_bound}{\treeK}). If $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$ and $\statej{\mst{\sigma}{\cdot}{v}}{\capt{S}{C}}$ then $\caple{\Gamma}{\annot{v}}{C}$, and if $\statej{\mst{\sigma}{\cdot}{a}}{\capt{S}{C}}$ then $\caple{\Gamma}{\cset{\rt{a}}}{C}$.

\subsection{What is not proved}\label{sec:target-open}

Three examples in \lean{Captures/Frontend/StepFC.lean} run an unboxing step, two by \rn{E-UnboxRefl} and one by \rn{E-UnboxCast}, on states that no Lean statement types (decided). No example runs one from a typed state, and no Lean statement instantiates Theorem~\ref{thm:target-unbox} or \lean{returned\_capture\_bound}. The second speaks of a final state at a type, and \lean{Steps.typed} forgets the answer type along a run, so it says nothing yet about the answer of a program. Example~\ref{ex:target-member} is on paper. \lean{C7\_rejected} rules out one evidence term, and Corollary~\ref{cor:target-star} rules out all of them only over a typed store.
