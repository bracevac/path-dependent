% unit U4
\section{The Full \treeK Target}\label{sec:target}
% unit macros
\mathtgt

This section gives the whole target of \treeK. It adds to the fragment of Section~\ref{sec:mini} what \Rone adds to its own, now with capture sets, and three things more. They are capture names with capture members, a store in the theorem about capture evidence, and boxes. Figures~\ref{fig:target-syntax} to~\ref{fig:target-capev} give these additions, and their captions name the rules that are report~1's. Its theorems are proven in \treeK.

\paragraph{A program with a capability member.}
The reactor of Section~\ref{sec:cc-later} lets a handler of a \lstinline{GUIReactor} use \lstinline{ui} and \lstinline{log} and nothing else. The Lean's example C2 has this shape over two platform capabilities $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$. In the DOT of Figure~\ref{fig:source} it reads
\[
\src{\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\klet c = \lambda(x : \capt{\dmu{z}{\dcap{C}{\cempty}{\cset{\kappa_1, \kappa_2}} \wedge \dfld{\mathit{run}}{\capt{(\dall{w}{\Unit}{\Unit})}{\cset{z.C}}}}}{\cset{\kappa_1, \kappa_2}}). \\
\qquad \lambda(u : \Unit).\ \klet y = x.\mathit{run} \kin y\,u \kin \\
\klet o_1 = \dnew{z}{\ddeftyp{C}{\cset{\kappa_1}} \wedge \ddeftrm{\mathit{run}}{\lambda(u : \Unit).\,u}} \kin \\
\klet o_2 = \dnew{z}{\ddeftyp{C}{\cset{\kappa_2}} \wedge \ddeftrm{\mathit{run}}{\lambda(u : \Unit).\,u}} \kin
\klet \mathit{ga} = c\,o_1 \kin \klet \mathit{gb} = c\,o_2 \kin \mathit{gb}
\end{array}}
\]
and the parameter type of $c$ translates to the object type
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\mathit{Obj} = \mu(\self.\,[\,\plec{\cempty}{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}},\ \plec{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}}{\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}},\ \phas{\mathit{run}}, \\
\qquad\qquad \ple{\sel{\self}{\mathit{run}}}{\pit[w]{\Unit}{\Unit}},\ \plec{\cset{\sel{\self}{\mathit{run}}}}{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}}\,]) .
\end{array}
\]
Number its propositions from 0, with $\plec{C}{D}$ a capture inclusion. Propositions 0 and 1 bound the capture member $C$ by $\cempty$ and $\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}$, and 2 and 3 are report~1's field $\mathit{run}$. The new proposition 4 says that $\mathit{run}$'s closure captures at most $\sel{\self}{C}$. The promise is that a call of $\mathit{run}$ is charged to the member's upper bound. Example~\ref{ex:target-client} checks the charge, and Theorem~\ref{thm:target-canon} gives it meaning.

\subsection{What the full target adds}\label{sec:target-delta}

Report~1's inclusion evidence becomes shape inclusion $e$, and a type inclusion $\ev{capt}\,e\,f$ pairs it with capture inclusion $f$. The function shape takes type inclusions (\rn{Sh-Pi}), so a cast checks capture sets on both sides of an arrow. Figures~\ref{fig:target-rules} and~\ref{fig:target-machine} give the new and changed rules and steps.

\paragraph{Capture names.}
No rule for terms adds a capture binder to a context, and no step allocates one. So the target, like DOT, abstracts over a capture set only through a member. Every label $\ell$ of the block of $x$ also names a capture set, $\sel{x}{\ell}$. For a capture member it is the member itself, and for a field the capture set declared for its value, which proposition 4 of $\mathit{Obj}$ bounds.

\paragraph{Recapture.}
The source types a variable $x$ at $\capt{S}{\cset{x}}$ (\rn{Var} of Section~\ref{sec:source-calc}). So a term that mentions $x$ records $x$ itself, and \rn{Sc-Var} widens $\cset{x}$ to $C$ only where it is needed. \rn{A-Var} gives $x$ the type $\capt{S}{C}$ of its binder. A cast would need evidence for $C \le \cset{x}$, and \rn{C-Var} gives only $\cset{x} \le C$. The atom $\ev{recap}\,a\,f$ keeps the shape of $a$ and takes any $C'$ with $f : \cset{\rt{a}} \le C'$ (\rn{A-Recap}), so $\ev{recap}\,x\,(\ev{refl}\,\cset{x})$ has type $\capt{S}{\cset{x}}$. Every wrapper keeps $\rt{a}$, so \rn{C-Var} and \rn{C-Member} read the same variable however an atom is wrapped.

\paragraph{Literals.}
A field runs when it is projected (\rn{E-Proj}), as the body of a closure runs when it is called. So a literal carries an annotation $A$, and each field carries closing evidence that its uses lie within $A$ and the self (\rn{F-Cons}). A projection has the type $\capt{(\sel{\rt{a}}{\ell})}{\cset{\sel{\rt{a}}{\ell}}}$ (\rn{T-Proj}).

\paragraph{Head forms.}
The steps read two head forms that report~1's normal forms lack. They are $\form{eqv}\,\varphi$ for closed equality evidence, which report~1 folds into $\form{id}$, and $\form{boxed}\,d$ for an inclusion of boxes (Section~\ref{sec:mech-trees}).

\subsection{Capture names and capture members}\label{sec:target-members}

A literal's precise telescope $\ofLit{W, W^{c}}{\vec{\ell}}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:target-syntax}) adds one capture equality per capture witness. A store binder records the capture witnesses, which $\Gamma(x) \ni \ell \mathrel{:=_{c}} C$ reads (Figure~\ref{fig:target-capev}).

Figure~\ref{fig:target-capev} gives capture evidence in full. \rn{C-Member} eliminates a capture inclusion at an atom, as report~1's $\ev{member}$ eliminates an inclusion, and \rn{CE-Def} reads a capture name of a transparent binder. A capture template $\ev{leC}\,p\,H\,p'$ reads a capture proposition through the hole $H$ and widens it by the sides $p$ and $p'$, lists of steps (\rn{M-LeC}). A $\ev{closed}$ step holds evidence typed outside the object, so its sets cannot mention the self. An $\ev{incl}$ step is a syntactic inclusion and may mention it.

\begin{example}[The reactor's client]\label{ex:target-client}
The client $c$ of C2 reads $\mathit{run}$ off its parameter $x$ and calls it. In the target its body is typed in $\Gamma_x = \kappa_1 : \cstar, \kappa_2 : \cstar, x : \capt{\mathit{Obj}}{\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}}, u : \Unit$. Write $h_2$, $e_3$, $f_4$ and $f_1$ for $\ev{member}\,x\,(\ev{refl}\,\mathit{Obj})\,i$ at $i = 2, 3, 4, 1$, which read those propositions of $\mathit{Obj}$ at $x$. The body is
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\klet y = \ev{cast}\,(\ev{proj}\,x\,\mathit{run}\,h_2)\,(\ev{capt}\,e_3\,f_4) \kin y\,u \\
\qquad \ann{\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2};\ \ev{union}\,(\ev{trans}\,(\ev{capvar}\,y)\,f_1)\,(\ev{trans}\,(\ev{capvar}\,u)\,(\ev{elem}\,\cempty\,\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}))} .
\end{array}
\]
\rn{T-Proj} and the cast give $y$ the type $\capt{(\pit[w]{\Unit}{\Unit})}{\cset{\sel{x}{C}}}$. The call uses $\cset{y, u}$, and the avoidance evidence charges it. \rn{C-Var} puts $\cset{y}$ below $\cset{\sel{x}{C}}$, and $f_1$ puts $\cset{\sel{x}{C}}$ below its upper bound $\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}$. The body has type $\Unit$ (\lean{C2\_client}, decided). It is checked once, for every object of type $\mathit{Obj}$.
\end{example}

\subsection{Roots with names}\label{sec:target-roots}

The roots of Figure~\ref{fig:target-capev} add one clause to those of Figure~\ref{fig:mini-syntax}, the name clause: a capture name follows the capture witness of its binder. A witness lives under its self, so it may name another capture name of the same block, and two names can define each other. The recursion then need not end. So roots spend one unit of fuel at each capture name, and $c$ is a root of $C$ when it is one at some fuel. A cycle contributes no root, as report~1's cyclic alias resolves to $\top$. A capture name of an opaque binder has no roots, and over a store every term binder is transparent. Over the binders of Figure~\ref{fig:target-syntax} every root is a star binder. When C2 stores its first literal at $o_1$, its binder records $\Gamma(o_1) \ni C \mathrel{:=_{c}} \cset{\kappa_1}$ and, from the set $\cset{z.C}$ at which the literal types its field, $\Gamma(o_1) \ni \mathit{run} \mathrel{:=_{c}} \cset{\sel{o_1}{C}}$. So $\cset{\sel{o_1}{\mathit{run}}}$ has the root $\kappa_1$ at fuel 2, and $\cset{o_1}$ has none (on paper).

\subsection{Boxes}\label{sec:target-boxes}

Boxing hides a capture set and unboxing recovers it (Section~\ref{sec:cc-later}). A box is a value, $\ev{box}\,a : \capt{(\boxt{T})}{\cempty}$ (\rn{V-Box}), so a container of capabilities can be pure, and a location that holds a box has no roots. Unboxing is a term, $\unboxc{a}{U}{f}$, with the charge $f : C \le U$ of the boxed set $C$ against the declared set $U$ (\rn{T-Unbox}). The boxed set is part of the type of $a$, not of the term. So the unboxing declares $U$, and its use set $\cset{\rt{a}} \cup U$ is read off the syntax. The set $D$ of the box plays no part. \rn{E-UnboxRefl} hands back the stored atom when $a$'s chain of casts has the head form $\form{id}$ or $\form{eqv}\,\varphi$, a bare variable included. \rn{E-UnboxCast} casts it by $d$ at $\form{boxed}\,d$, as \rn{E-AppCast} casts an argument.

C7 is a pure container of two boxed capabilities, at the object type
\[
\mathit{Pair} = \obj{\tel_1 \mathbin{+\!\!+} \tel_2} \quad\text{with}\quad \tel_i = [\,\phas{\ell_i},\ \ple{\sel{\self}{\ell_i}}{\boxt{\CapT{\kappa_i}}},\ \plec{\cset{\sel{\self}{\ell_i}}}{\cempty}\,] .
\]
In $\kappa_1 : \cstar, \kappa_2 : \cstar, o : \capt{\mathit{Pair}}{\cempty}$ the client binds the field $\ell_1$ of $o$ to $y : \capt{(\boxt{\CapT{\kappa_1}})}{\cempty}$ and returns $\unboxc{y}{\cset{\kappa_1}}{\ev{refl}\,\cset{\kappa_1}}$ in a $\kw{let}$ that declares $\cset{\kappa_1}$. It has type $\CapT{\kappa_1}$ (\lean{C7\_client}, decided). It uses $\cset{o, \kappa_1}$, whose roots are $\cset{\kappa_1}$. So $\kappa_2$ never enters, although $o$ holds both capabilities (on paper).

\subsection{Canonical forms of closed capture evidence}\label{sec:target-canon}

Report~1's canonical forms (its Theorem~\ref{R1-thm:canon}) assume a typed store and normalize closed evidence to a typed head form. Capture evidence gets no head form, and its canonical form is a fact about roots, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:mini-cap}. That theorem held in every context. With capture members it cannot. The client of Example~\ref{ex:target-client} read its bound off the type of $x$, and a type can state a false bound. Under a binder of type $\obj{[\,\plec{\cset{\kappa}}{\cempty}\,]}$ one $\ev{member}$ proves $\cset{\kappa} \le \cempty$, although $\kappa$ is a root of $\cset{\kappa}$ and not of $\cempty$ (Example~\ref{ex:target-bad}). Over a typed store the theorem holds again.

\paragraph{Why a store helps.}
Over a typed store every capture proposition of the type of an atom $a$, read at $\rt{a}$, holds between the roots of its two sides. The bad binder's $\plec{\cset{\kappa}}{\cempty}$ does not. Three facts give this. Every atom is a chain of casts on a location that holds a value. A literal stored at $y$ asserts $\peqc{\cset{\sel{\self}{\ell}}}{W^{c}(\ell)}$ for each capture witness, and at $y$ both sides have the same roots by the name clause. A cast by $\ev{obj}\,\tel\,m$ proves each proposition of its target by a template in $m$, from the proposition of $\tel$ that the template's hole names (Section~\ref{R1-sec:mini-evidence} of report~1). The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:target-canon} reads the steps of a capture template's sides as inclusions of roots. Report~1 records such facts in a typed view $\viewj{\Gamma}{r}{\sigma}{V}{\tel}$, one form of $V$ per proposition of $\tel$ at the variable $r$ over $\sigma$ (its Figure~\ref{R1-fig:formtyped-views}). The capture trees add two capture forms without data, $\form{leC}$ and $\form{eqC}$, whose typedness is the fact about roots, with $\inst{C}{r}$ for $C[\self \mapsto r]$:
\begin{mathpar}
\inferrule[View-LeC]{\viewj{\Gamma}{r}{\sigma}{V}{\tel} \\ \caple{\Gamma}{\inst{C_1}{r}}{\inst{C_2}{r}}}{\viewj{\Gamma}{r}{\sigma}{\tcons{V}{\form{leC}}}{\tcons{\tel}{\plec{C_1}{C_2}}}}
\and
\inferrule[View-EqC]{\viewj{\Gamma}{r}{\sigma}{V}{\tel} \\ \capeqv{\Gamma}{\inst{C_1}{r}}{\inst{C_2}{r}}}{\viewj{\Gamma}{r}{\sigma}{\tcons{V}{\form{eqC}}}{\tcons{\tel}{\peqc{C_1}{C_2}}}}
\end{mathpar}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical forms of closed capture evidence, \leanin{cap\_canon}{\treeK}]\label{thm:target-canon}
Let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\clej{\Gamma}{f}{C}{D}$ then $\caple{\Gamma}{C}{D}$.
\item If $\ceqj{\Gamma}{\psi}{C}{D}$ then $\capeqv{\Gamma}{C}{D}$ (\lean{capeq\_canon}).
\item If $\atomj{\Gamma}{a}{\capt{S}{C}}$ then $\caple{\Gamma}{\cset{\rt{a}}}{C}$ (the capture conjunct of \lean{atom\_canon}).
\item If $\lej{\Gamma}{d}{\capt{S}{C}}{\capt{S'}{C'}}$ then $\caple{\Gamma}{C}{C'}$ (\lean{le\_canon\_cap}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A mutual induction on derivations proves it and report~1's canonical forms at shapes.
\begin{itemize}
\item \rn{C-Refl}, \rn{C-Trans}, \rn{C-Elem} and \rn{C-Union} as in Theorem~\ref{thm:mini-cap}. \rn{C-Var} is item 3, and \rn{C-EqToLe} is item 2.
\item \rn{C-Member} and \rn{CE-Member}, the only cases that read the store. Report~1's theorem gives the view of $a$ and the form of $e$, and pushes the view through the form to a typed view of the target telescope. Its form at $i$ is a capture form, and its typedness is the conclusion, $\caple{\Gamma}{C_1[\self \mapsto \rt{a}]}{C_2[\self \mapsto \rt{a}]}$, or $\approx_\Gamma$ for an equality.
\item \rn{CE-Refl}, \rn{CE-Symm} and \rn{CE-Trans}: $\approx_\Gamma$ is an equivalence. \rn{CE-Def}: a defined capture name has the roots of its witness, by the name clause.
\item Item 3. \rn{A-Var} is Lemma~\ref{lem:mini-var}, now at every fuel. \rn{A-Cast} is items 3 and 4, \rn{A-Fold}, \rn{A-Unfold} and \rn{A-Both} keep the capture set, and \rn{A-Recap} is item 1. Item 4. \rn{L-Capt} is item 1 at its capture half. \qedhere
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A capture member through a literal, on paper]\label{ex:target-member}
When C2 calls $c\,o_1$, the parameter $x$ of the client becomes a cast of the stored literal. Then $f_1$ of Example~\ref{ex:target-client} is closed evidence. Here is how Theorem~\ref{thm:target-canon} reads it, for that literal without its field, $\litc{\cempty}{\tnil}{C = \cset{\kappa_1}}{\tnil}$ at $o_1$, over $\kappa_1 : \cstar, \kappa_2 : \cstar$. Its context $\Gamma$ types $o_1$ at $\capt{(\obj{\tel_o})}{\cempty}$ with $\tel_o = [\,\peqc{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}}{\cset{\kappa_1}}\,]$. The translation $\mathit{Abs}$ of the capture member of $\mathit{Obj}$, and templates $m$ from $\tel_o$ to it, are
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\mathit{Abs} = \obj{[\,\plec{\cempty}{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}},\ \plec{\cset{\sel{\self}{C}}}{\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}}\,]} , \\
m = [\,\ev{leC}\,(\ev{incl}\,\cempty\,\cset{\kappa_1} :: [\,])\,(\ev{eqSymC}\,0)\,[\,],\ \ev{leC}\,[\,]\,(\ev{eqC}\,0)\,(\ev{incl}\,\cset{\kappa_1}\,\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2} :: [\,])\,] .
\end{array}
\]
The first template reads the equality backwards (\rn{H-EqSymC}) and widens $\cempty$ to $\cset{\kappa_1}$ on its left. The second reads it forwards (\rn{H-EqC}) and widens $\cset{\kappa_1}$ to $\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}$ on its right. So $a' = \ev{cast}\,o_1\,(\ev{capt}\,(\ev{obj}\,\tel_o\,m)\,(\ev{refl}\,\cempty))$ has type $\capt{\mathit{Abs}}{\cempty}$, and $\ev{member}\,a'\,(\ev{refl}\,\mathit{Abs})\,1$ proves $\cset{\sel{o_1}{C}} \le \cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}$. In the member case, the name clause types the capture form at position 0 by $\capeqv{\Gamma}{\cset{\sel{o_1}{C}}}{\cset{\kappa_1}}$. The second template passes it through $\cset{\kappa_1} \subseteq \cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}$. So $\caple{\Gamma}{\cset{\sel{o_1}{C}}}{\cset{\kappa_1,\kappa_2}}$.
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}[Closed evidence keeps a star binder, \leanin{Store.Typed.no\_cap\_escape}{\treeK}]\label{cor:target-star}
Let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$ and $\Gamma(\kappa) = \cstar$. If $\kappa \notin \roots{\Gamma}{D}$ then no $f$ has $\clej{\Gamma}{f}{\cset{\kappa}}{D}$. In particular no $f$ has $\clej{\Gamma}{f}{\cset{\kappa}}{\cempty}$ (\lean{Store.Typed.no\_cap\_star\_le\_nil}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\kappa$ is a root of $\cset{\kappa}$, so item 1 of Theorem~\ref{thm:target-canon} would make it a root of $D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Bad capture bounds, revisited]\label{ex:target-bad}
C3 makes the point of Section~\ref{sec:problem-bad} with an unboxing where that closure calls $\mathit{log}$. It is typed over one star binder $\kappa : \cstar$, and its outer binder has the type $\mathit{Bad} = \obj{[\,\plec{\cset{\kappa}}{\cempty}\,]}$. This states in one proposition the bad inclusion that the binder of Section~\ref{sec:problem-bad} states through $\sel{\self}{C}$ in two.
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\lambda^{\cempty}(x : \capt{\mathit{Bad}}{\cempty}).\,\lambda^{\cempty}(z : \capt{(\boxt{\capt{\top}{\cset{\kappa}}})}{\cempty}).\,\unboxc{z}{\cempty}{\ev{member}\,x\,(\ev{refl}\,\mathit{Bad})\,0} \\
\qquad \ann{\ev{elem}\,\cset{z}\,\cset{z}}\ \ann{\ev{elem}\,\cempty\,\cset{x}}
\end{array}
\]
The inner closure has the empty annotation and returns a value of type $\capt{\top}{\cset{\kappa}}$. Its typing is decided (\lean{C3\_typed}), and so is $\ev{member}\,x\,(\ev{refl}\,\mathit{Bad})\,0 : \cset{\kappa} \le \cempty$ under both binders (\lean{C3\_badBounds}). The unboxing takes $\kappa$ off the boxed value and charges $\cempty$. Over a typed store no atom $a$ has a type $\capt{\mathit{Bad}}{D}$, since $\ev{member}\,a\,(\ev{refl}\,\mathit{Bad})\,0$ would be closed evidence that Corollary~\ref{cor:target-star} rules out. A call of the outer lambda from a typed state would hand its body such an atom, so the body never runs. Both steps are on paper, by report~1's argument for bad bounds with roots in place of head forms.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[Type safety and erasure, \lean{preservation'}, \lean{progress}, \lean{erase\_step} and \lean{erase\_reflect'} in \treeK]\label{thm:target-meta}

\begin{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Effect safety never flags an unboxing, since the variable $\rt{a}$ it inspects holds a box and has no roots. The next theorem speaks of report~1's root $\rt{b}$ of the atom $b$ it hands back.

\begin{theorem}[Effect safety at an unboxing, \leanin{effect\_safety\_unbox}{\treeK}]\label{thm:target-unbox}
Let $\statej{\mathit{st}}{T}$, let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$ for the store $\sigma$ of $\mathit{st}$, and let $\kappa \notin \roots{\Gamma}{\uses{\mathit{st}}}$. Let $\mathit{st} \steps \mathit{st}'$ with $\mathit{st}' = \mst{\sigma'}{K'}{\unboxc{a}{U}{f}}$ and $\sigma'(\rt{a}) = \ev{box}\,b$, and let $\storej{\sigma'}{\Gamma'}$. Then $\sext{\sigma}{\sigma'}$ and $\kappa \notin \roots{\Gamma'}{\cset{\rt{b}}}$.
\end{theorem}

Its proof takes the unboxing step from progress and applies capture prediction to the longer run. No Lean statement instantiates it (Section~\ref{sec:source-open}).
