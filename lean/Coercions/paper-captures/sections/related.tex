\section{Related Work and What Is Left Out}\label{sec:related}
\mathplain

\paragraph{Capturing types.}
Boruch-Gruszecki et al.\ \cite{boruchgruszecki2023capturing} introduce $\mathrm{CC}_{<:\square}$, a calculus in the style of System~F$_{<:}$ with capturing types, subcapturing, boxes and unboxing, and scoped capabilities. The sources here have the same boxes and the same reading of $\capt{S}{C}$. The setting differs. Their types have no type members, and ours are the types of DOT, in which a type can name a member of a variable. Safer exceptions for Scala \cite{odersky2021safer} designs checked exceptions as capabilities. The \lean{Try} example of Section~\ref{sec:classifiers} is a model of the use that the reference documentation describes \cite{scala3cc}.

\paragraph{What's in the Box.}
Xu et al.\ \cite{xu2025box} give Capless, a core calculus with bounded quantification over captures, existential types and a typing judgment indexed by a use set, and Reacap, a surface calculus with the universal capability and reach capabilities. A translation from Reacap to Capless is type preserving and proved on paper. The metatheory of Capless is mechanized in Lean~4. The judgment $\dtyuj{U}{\Gamma}{t}{T}$ and the reading of a closure's annotation as a bound on the use set of its body follow Capless. The departures are these.
\begin{itemize}
\item Capless has both rules \lean{cinstl} and \lean{cinstr} for an instance binder. The source of \treeCC and \treeCL has only \rn{Sc-Inst}, $\subcj{\Gamma}{C}{\cset{\kappa}}$, which is \lean{Subcap.inst}. The target's \rn{CE-Inst} is an equality, so the target derives both directions.
\item \treeK has no universal capability. A result $\cany$ reads as a closed set. \treeCC and \treeCL have $\topc$ as an atom.
\item Boxes are kept in the source and the target. The core of Capless has none.
\item Capture members of objects stand where Capless has abstraction over captures.
\item In the target a $\kw{let}$ or an unboxing declares a use set, with evidence that erases, and the use set of a term is computed from its syntax. The source, like Capless, has a subsumption rule on use sets (\lean{HasTy.sub}).
\item In \treeCC and \treeCL packing is an answer inclusion, \rn{Ans-Pack}, with evidence that the witness is below a declared bound. The pack wrapper on atoms and values carries the same evidence and is typed only at an existential answer. The pack term of Capless carries a witness and a variable, and its existential has no bound.
\end{itemize}
One departure is from the compiler and not from Capless. In \treeCC and \treeCL a call instantiates a parameter $\cany$ at the argument, where the compiler creates a fresh capability per call. Reach capabilities were dropped from Scala on 2026-06-16 (compiler commit \lean{9521ae1b0b}), and the formal foundation for them \cite{xu2024reach} is not modelled here. The reading of the language follows the reference documentation at commit \lean{4dae25087d} \cite{scala3cc}. The documentation is not a theorem. No theorem relates the typer of Section~\ref{sec:frontends} to the compiler.

\paragraph{Classifying capabilities.}
Pham et al.\ \cite{pham2026classifying} add a tree of classifiers to Capless, the projections $\konly{k}$ and $\kexcept{k}$, and a kind algebra, and call the result Capless(K). They prove safety of a checked big-step semantics, mechanized in Lean~4, and derive type safety, effect safety, used label prediction, capture prediction and handler coverage from it. \treeCL has classifiers, kinds and filtered capture sets. It lacks kind-bounded capture binders, the merge rule for projections, labels and handlers. Its prediction theorems are small-step statements over explicit stores. Their capture prediction bounds what the value that a term returns may use. Capture prediction in this report bounds the use set of every state that a run reaches. The words \emph{effect safety} mean something else in Capless(K), where a term typed with the empty use set and run with no label allowance answers with a value and not with a break. This report keeps the Lean's name for the theorem about the variable a step inspects.

\paragraph{Box inference and separation.}
Xu and Odersky formalize box inference for the capture calculus \cite{xu2023boxinference}. The typers of Section~\ref{sec:frontends} insert boxes and unboxings, and the documentation of the Lean attributes the algorithm to the compiler's adaptation of boxed types. No theorem relates the typers to either source. Degrees of Separation \cite{xu2024separation} adds separation checking to capture checking. System Capybara \cite{xu2026capybara} tracks separation, consumption, freshness and read-only access. It translates a surface calculus into CoreCapybara, an extension of Capless in which a result $\cfresh$ becomes an existential, and proves CoreCapybara sound with a semantic model in Lean~4. The separation checker of Scala~3 implements it. Separation was started as a fourth tree, a copy of \treeCC, and is parked on the branch \lean{separation} with two open machine-checked findings. No theorem of this report depends on it.

\paragraph{Report 1.}
This report builds on \Rone. Its calculus \FCdot, its normalizer and its canonical forms theorem are the base of the three trees, and its related work on DOT, on decidability and on explicit coercions applies unchanged. The calculi above are not path dependent. Capture checking over DOT is the setting of this report.

\paragraph{What is left out.}
The trees do not model the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item Reach capabilities, dropped from the language.
\item An $\cany$ under a box, in a type-member bound or in the lower bound of a capture member. \treeCC and \treeCL refuse it too.
\item Separation checking, parked.
\item The \lean{Unscoped} and shared classifiers that the compiler's level check consults.
\item Mutability, control effects and kind-bounded capture binders.
\item The parameter $\cany$ in \treeK. A program writes a capture member instead.
\item Recursive subtyping, which report~1 also leaves out \cite{rompf2016soundness}.
\end{itemize}
