Scala's capture checker can also say what kind of capabilities a value may use. A capability class may extend a classifier, such as \lean{Control}, and a capture set can keep only the capabilities of one classifier, or exclude them. This section adds classifiers to \treeCC, as the tree \treeCL does: kinds, filtered sets and where the filter must act, kinding evidence with its canonical form, and the run-time theorems with their instances. Every theorem of this section is in \treeCL.

\subsection{Programs and promises}\label{sec:cl-programs}

The two signatures of Section~\ref{sec:cc-later} show the two uses of a classifier. The object that \lean{Try.apply} returns drops the capabilities of \lean{body} known to be unrelated to \lean{Control} and keeps the rest, unclassified ones included (\texttt{classifiers.md}, lines 105 to 113). The argument of \lean{runOnNewThread} may capture only capabilities known to be unrelated to \lean{Control}, since a \lean{Control} capability is bound to the stack of the thread that created it (lines 126 to 149).

\treeCL models both after Capless(K) \cite{pham2026classifying}. Its E1 is \lean{Try.apply} with the result filtered to the kind $\konly{\mathit{Control}}$. Its E2 is Capless(K)'s \lean{Future.apply}, whose body is kinded at $\kexcept{\mathit{ThreadLocal}}$. The Lean has no \lean{runOnNewThread} at $\kexcept{\mathit{Control}}$, and in both models the program never calls the body. The promise the Lean keeps is this: if the use set of a program is kinded at $\chi$, every leaf of a variable that a run inspects has a classifier that $\chi$ admits (Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-effect}).

A classifier is a node of a tree, built from indices: $\top$ is the top node and $\clchild{n}{k}$ the $n$-th child of $k$ (Figure~\ref{fig:cl-syntax}). The order $k \le k'$ says that $k$ lies in the subtree of $k'$. The examples use the tree of Capless(K), with $\mathit{Control}$ below $\mathit{ThreadLocal}$ and $\mathit{IO}$ apart. The Scala reference puts \lean{Control} below \lean{SharedCapability} instead (\texttt{classifiers.md}, line 51).

A kind is a union of subtrees, each a classifier minus excluded subtrees, and its operations are Boolean functions. Subtraction removes the subtrees of $\chi'$ from $\chi$ one at a time. \treeCL proves only its sound half: a classifier that $\chi$ contains and $\chi'$ does not is contained in $\chi \setminus \chi'$ (\leanin{Kind.contains\_subtract\_of}{\treeCL}). So $\subkind{\chi}{\chi'}$ and $\kcont{k}{\chi}$ give $\kcont{k}{\chi'}$ (\lean{Kind.Subkind.contains}), the only fact about subkinding the development uses. The converse is not proved, so the subkind test is not known to be reflexive or transitive, and a template that relates a kind to itself takes the step $\admstep{\chi}{\chi'}$.

\subsection{Filtered capture sets and their leaves}\label{sec:cl-filter}

The atom $\restr{c}{\chi}$ is Scala's \lean{c.only[A]}, or \lean{c.except[A]} with an exclusion kind. It is data. Resolution carries the filter along, and expansion applies it last. An atom with no declared classifier has classifier $\top$, which $\konly{\mathit{Control}}$ excludes and $\kexcept{k}$ admits for every $k \neq \top$ (\lean{CapBound.classifier}). Scala reads an unclassified capability the other way: \lean{c.only[A]} keeps it and \lean{c.except[A]} refuses it. In Example~\ref{ex:cl-k6} the kinding rules of Section~\ref{sec:cl-kinding} refuse it at an exclusion too. Classified binders $\kappa : \clsb{k}$ sit on the platform only. Store extension needs a binder that is not opaque, so no step allocates one (\lean{CapBound.cls\_not\_appendable}).

\begin{lemma}[Leaves of a filtered set, \leanin{Ctx.roots\_proj}{\treeCL}]
For every $\Gamma$, $n$, $C$ and $\chi$, $\leavesn{\Gamma}{n}{\restr{C}{\chi}} = \cset{b \in \leavesn{\Gamma}{n}{C} \mid \kcont{\clsof{\Gamma}{b}}{\chi}}$.
\end{lemma}

The filter must act after expansion. Acting inside resolution, it would admit a scope capability that the expansion after it opens into binders the filter excludes. Section~\ref{sec:history} records that design.

\begin{example}[Filtered leaves]\label{ex:cl-filter}
Over $\kappa_{\mathit{ctl}} : \clsb{\mathit{Control}},\ \kappa_{\mathit{io}} : \clsb{\mathit{IO}}$, the leaves of $\cset{\restr{\kappa_{\mathit{ctl}}}{\konly{\mathit{Control}}}}$ are $\cset{\kappa_{\mathit{ctl}}}$ (\lean{K1x\_roots\_ctl}), and $\cset{\restr{\kappa_{\mathit{io}}}{\konly{\mathit{Control}}}}$ has none (\lean{K1x\_roots\_io}). Over $\kappa_{\mathit{tl}} : \clsb{\mathit{ThreadLocal}},\ \varrho : \scopeb,\ \kappa_{\mathit{ctl}} : \clsb{\mathit{Control}}$, the leaves of $\cset{\restr{\varrho}{\kexcept{\mathit{ThreadLocal}}}}$ are $\topc$ and $\varrho$ (\lean{K3x\_roots}). All three are built by hand.
\end{example}

A kinding judgment $\kindj{\Gamma}{g}{C}{\chi}$ says that $g$ shows every leaf of $C$ to have a classifier in $\chi$ (Figure~\ref{fig:cl-kinding}). \rn{Kd-Proj} and \rn{Kd-Cls} each merge two rules of Capless(K). \rn{Kd-Proj} kinds an atom whose own kind is below $\chi$, so at a bare atom $\chi$ must admit every classifier. \rn{Kd-Cls} kinds a classified binder whose classifier $\chi$ contains, or that the atom's own filter excludes, and it never reads the $\top$ of a star binder (Example~\ref{ex:cl-k6}). \rn{Kd-Member} reads a kinding proposition off an object type, as \rn{C-Member} reads a capture inclusion.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical forms of closed kinding evidence, \leanin{kind\_canon}{\treeCL}]\label{thm:cl-kind}
Let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$. If $\kindj{\Gamma}{g}{C}{\chi}$, then $\kindle{\Gamma}{C}{\chi}$: every leaf of $C$ in $\Gamma$ has a classifier in $\chi$.
\end{theorem}

The theorem needs no member-free hypothesis. It is proved inside \treeCL's copy of the mutual induction behind Theorem~\ref{thm:target-canon}, since \rn{Kd-Var} and \rn{Kd-Member} read atom typings and \rn{C-Proj} reads kinding. The three capture rules add cases to \lean{cap\_canon}, each the lemma above read one way. A checker decides kinding evidence, and \leanin{checkKindCo\_iff\_hasType}{\treeCL} says that it accepts exactly the derivable evidence. That is adequacy for the rules, not completeness of the rules (Example~\ref{ex:cl-k6}).

\subsection{Classified prediction and effect safety}\label{sec:cl-runtime}

The run-time theorems need no new induction on runs. Kinding is antitone along $\preccurlyeq_{\Gamma}$ (\lean{Ctx.KindLe.mono}). It survives store extension, since extension keeps every binder's classifier: if $\sextr{\rho}{\sigma}{\sigma'}$, $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$, $\storej{\sigma'}{\Gamma'}$ and $\kindle{\Gamma}{C}{\chi}$, then $\kindle{\Gamma'}{\rho(C)}{\chi}$ (\lean{Store.Ext.kindLe}). Capture prediction, Theorem~\ref{thm:mini-pred} in \treeCL's copy, supplies the inclusion.

\begin{theorem}[Classified prediction and effect safety, \leanin{classified\_prediction}{\treeCL} and \leanin{classified\_effect\_safety}{\treeCL}]\label{thm:cl-effect}
Let $\statej{\mathit{st}}{T}$, let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$ for the store $\sigma$ of $\mathit{st}$, and let $\kindle{\Gamma}{\uses{\mathit{st}}}{\chi}$. Let $\mathit{st} \steps \mathit{st}'$, with store $\sigma'$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item There is $\rho$ with $\sextr{\rho}{\sigma}{\sigma'}$ such that every $\Gamma'$ with $\storej{\sigma'}{\Gamma'}$ has $\caple{\Gamma'}{\uses{\mathit{st}'}}{\rho(\uses{\mathit{st}})}$ and $\kindle{\Gamma'}{\uses{\mathit{st}'}}{\chi}$.
\item If $\insp{\mathit{st}'} = x$ and $\storej{\sigma'}{\Gamma'}$, then $\kindle{\Gamma'}{\cset{x}}{\chi}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Item~2 follows from item~1 by \lean{inspects\_covered}.

\begin{theorem}[Classified effect safety for \DOTMNF, \leanin{dot\_classified\_effect\_safety'}{\treeCL}]\label{thm:cl-source}
Let $\plat$ be a platform, $\src{d}$ a derivation of $\dtyuj{U}{\platctx}{t}{T}$, and $\src{\platctx \vdash g : U :: \chi}$ a source kinding derivation. Let $\mst{\platstore}{\cdot}{\src{t}} \steps \src{\mathit{st}}$ with $\insp{\src{\mathit{st}}} = x$. Then there are a target state $\tstate$ with store $\sigma'$, a context $\Gamma'$ and a renaming $\rho$ such that $\mst{\tplatstore}{\cdot}{\trans{d}} \steps \tstate$, $\tstate$ is typed, $\insp{\tstate} = x$, $\erase{\tstate} = \erase{\src{\mathit{st}}}$, $\storej{\sigma'}{\Gamma'}$, $\sextr{\rho}{\tplatstore}{\sigma'}$, and $\kindle{\Gamma'}{\cset{x}}{\chi}$.
\end{theorem}

The translation of $\src{g}$ is typed kinding evidence (\leanin{CapKind.translate\_typed}{\treeCL}), so Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-kind} at the platform's store gives $\kindle{\trans{\platctx}}{\trans{U}}{\chi}$. That is the hypothesis of the unprimed \lean{dot\_classified\_effect\_safety}, proved as Theorem~\ref{thm:source-effect} with Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-effect} in place of effect safety.

\treeCL's \lean{dot\_effect\_safety} takes the semantic hypothesis on $\kappa$ (Section~\ref{sec:source-thms}). The syntactic one would make it false: $\kappa_{\mathit{ctl}}$ is no atom of $\cset{\restr{\kappa_{\mathit{ctl}}}{\konly{\mathit{Control}}}}$ and is its leaf (Example~\ref{ex:cl-filter}). For the same reason \treeCL's \lean{Platform.root\_iff} asks that no atom of the set be filtered.

\subsection{Instances}\label{sec:cl-instances}

\begin{example}[Try and Future, run]\label{ex:cl-try}
E1 runs on the platform $\kappa_{\mathit{ctl}} : \clsb{\mathit{Control}},\ \kappa_{\mathit{io}} : \clsb{\mathit{IO}}$ and is
\[
\src{\klet b = \lambda(u : \Unit).\,u \kin \klet f = \mathit{Try.apply} \kin \klet r = f\,b \kin r} ,
\]
with $b$ and the program's use set at $\restr{\cset{\kappa_{\mathit{ctl}}, \kappa_{\mathit{io}}}}{\konly{\mathit{Control}}}$. \lean{E1\_typed} and the kinding \lean{E1\_kind} are built by hand with decided side conditions. The run inspects only $f$, which holds $\mathit{Try.apply}$, a closure annotated $\cempty$. So the inspected variable has no leaf (\lean{E1\_reads\_pure}, by hand), and the instance \lean{E1\_effect\_safety} of Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-source} (by hand) holds for want of a leaf. E2 is the same for $\mathit{Future.apply}$ (\lean{E2\_reads\_pure} and \lean{E2\_effect\_safety}, by hand).

The informative instances do not call $\mathit{Try.apply}$ or $\mathit{Future.apply}$. E1r is
\[
\src{\klet u = \lambda(x : \Unit).\,x \kin \klet b = \lambda(x : \Unit).\,x \kin \klet r = b\,u \kin r}
\]
with $b$ at the filtered platform set, and its run inspects $b$ at the call. \lean{E1r\_read\_has\_root} applies \lean{E1r\_effect\_safety}, the instance of Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-source}, at the source state of the call (both by hand). It adds that $\kappa_{\mathit{ctl}}$ is a leaf of $b$ in the matched target state (\lean{E1r\_reads\_root}, by hand). So the theorem speaks of a leaf that exists, and it rules out $\kappa_{\mathit{io}}$, which the filter dropped. E2r does the same at $\kexcept{\mathit{ThreadLocal}}$ with the leaf $\kappa_{\mathit{io}}$ (\lean{E2r\_read\_has\_root}, by hand). The refusal of a thread-local body is a fact about kinding: no source kinding derivation puts E2's thread-local capability at $\kexcept{\mathit{ThreadLocal}}$ (\lean{E2\_tl\_not\_capKind}, by hand).
\end{example}

\begin{example}[An incompleteness]\label{ex:cl-k6}
Let $\Gamma = \kappa_{\mathit{tl}} : \clsb{\mathit{ThreadLocal}},\ \kappa_p : \cstar$. The star binder $\kappa_p$ declares no classifier, is its own only leaf, and has classifier $\top$, which $\kexcept{\mathit{ThreadLocal}}$ contains. So $\kindle{\Gamma}{\cset{\kappa_p}}{\kexcept{\mathit{ThreadLocal}}}$ (\lean{K6x\_kindLe}, by hand), and yet no evidence derives it (\lean{K6x\_no\_kindCo}, by hand). \rn{Kd-Cls} needs a declared classifier, and \rn{Kd-Proj} needs $\subkind{\konly{\top}}{\kexcept{\mathit{ThreadLocal}}}$, which fails, and the checker refuses both, decided. For all evidence, the proof renames $\kappa_p$ to the instance binder $\kappa_p \instb{\cset{\kappa_{\mathit{tl}}}}$, along which kinding evidence travels. In the image the only leaf of $\cset{\kappa_p}$ is $\kappa_{\mathit{tl}}$, which the kind excludes, and a store types the image, so Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-kind} refutes the evidence. A rule that read $\top$ off a star binder would derive this kinding and so lose its renaming lemma (Section~\ref{sec:history}).
\end{example}

\subsection{What is not proved}\label{sec:cl-open}

No theorem says that the Future program with a thread-local argument has no typing derivation, only that it has no kinding derivation (\lean{E2\_tl\_not\_capKind}). The model of $\mathit{Try.apply}$ never runs its body, since the field of the object it returns holds a fresh identity closure. Subtraction is proved sound, not complete, and subkinding is not known to be a preorder. Kinding is incomplete at a star binder, and the filter and the leaves read such a binder the other way from Scala. Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-kind} speaks of contexts that a store types, so it says nothing about kinding inside a lambda body, and the source theorems apply it at the platform only. Capless(K)'s kind-bounded capture binders, its merge of subsets, and its labels and handlers are not modelled (Section~\ref{sec:related}).
