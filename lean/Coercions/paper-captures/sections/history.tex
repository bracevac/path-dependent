\section{Designs That Did Not Work}\label{sec:history}
\mathplain

Several parts of the three trees look arbitrary until one sees what they replaced. This section records the designs that were tried and dropped and the theorems that were found to say nothing. Table~\ref{tab:history} summarizes them with the kind of evidence. MC is a machine-checked counterexample, a Lean file that the kernel accepted. A is an argument made on paper, usually by a reviewer who attacked the design before it was built. C is a cost judgment: the design would work and was refused for its price. A refusal of kind C, or with no kind, is a decision and not a refutation. The counterexample files and the design notes are not in the repository. They are in the author's archive of 2026-10-16, so the trees hold only the designs that survived.

\subsection{Where capture sets live}

Option (a) puts capture binders and an existential type in the target, as Capless has them. Option (b) puts the capture set of an object under its self binder, as a proposition $\plec{\cset{\self}}{C}$ of its telescope. Option (c) keeps the capture set outside the shape, as $\capt{S}{C}$, reads capture quantification as a capture member of an object, and reads a result $\cany$ as a closed set. Option (c) won. Option (a) fails on the existential result of the iterator example C5. At the caller no rule fires on an unbounded capture binder, so the avoidance evidence for the result does not exist (A). It also needs a second family of normal forms for answers (C). Option (b) works on paper, but a closed side of a capture template cannot bridge two sets that mention the self. Templates become chains of hole indices, which reaches the checker, the normalizer and the composition of entries (C). Four reviewers then attacked option (c) as first written. One finding: the sides of a capture template were to be typed at a root mode, and the side typing of \Rone has no mode (A). Option (a) came back for one use. \treeCC gives $\cfresh$ an existential with a declared bound (Section~\ref{sec:levels}).

\subsection{Boxes three times}

The first design made a box a capture name on a label. A container holding $\dbox{f}$ is pure in the source, but the target's field condition forces the literal's annotation to contain the root of $f$, so the translation is not typed (A). A witness at a label resolves through the store, so a boxed value under a label is still applicable. Inertness belongs to the shape, which led to the box shape $\boxt{T}$. The second design made a box an atom wrapper. Stage A1 refuted it in the kernel (MC). A type witness may mention the self inside a capture set. The rule $\ev{member}$ at an atom that unboxes a box gave $\cempty$ for the self, and resolution at the root gave $\cset{x}$, so \lean{eq\_canon} failed on a typed store. A box is now a value, stored like any literal, and an unboxing is a term (\lean{Value.box} and \lean{Tm.unbox} in \treeK). The third design erased a box to a one-field runtime object at a reserved label. Stage A3a refuted it (MC). A source literal with one term member and the box of that member erase to the same runtime object, and a projection and an unboxing erase to the same projection. The simulation ties states by erasure alone, so it cannot separate them, and one steps while the other is stuck. The runtime now has an inert box (\lean{Tm.box} and \lean{Tm.unbox} in \lean{Captures/Runtime.lean}).

\subsection{The parameter \texorpdfstring{$\cany$}{any} and tunneling, the DOT way}

\treeK reads no parameter $\cany$ and no tunneling, which means $\cany$ under a box, in a type-member bound or in the lower bound of a capture member. A parameter $\cany$ would be a capture member of a parameter object, allocated at each call. The target would then have an application per call that the source does not have, and erasure equality would be argued again at every call site (C and A). The reason for tunneling is deferral only. No counterexample exists for either. A program writes an explicit capture member instead. The refusal is not in the typing judgment. \lean{HasTy} does not mention the predicate \lean{AnyOk}, and the front end rejects the programs that fail it. \treeCC reads a parameter $\cany$ as the capture binder of the arrow and still refuses tunneling, so its \lean{withFile} example is monomorphic.

\subsection{The compiler's way}

The universal capability was first a binder at the head of the platform prefix. Reviewers found that two write-ups of that design contradicted each other and that it types the escape at top level (A). It is now an atom $\topc$ with the strictest level. Levels must survive renaming. Inserting a scope binder before another binder changes the level of the other, so level evidence about it becomes false (A). Renamings and substitutions now carry three capture fields. At stage B2 the kernel refuted the substitution lemma \lean{Subst.Typed.singleC} for an instance binder instantiated by $\topc$ (MC), and it now carries the premise \lean{b.instSet? = none}. Pack as a term, as in Capless, failed in a reviewer's reading. Preservation fails at allocation because an answer cast on a value is dropped, and progress fails on a packed atom (A, no counterexample file). Pack is now a subtyping step, \lean{ELeCo.pack}. Answer evidence with its own normal forms was refuted at stage B2 (MC). The lemmas \lean{castRedex\_steps} and \lean{castRedex\_normalize} have no typing hypothesis, so a step with a normalization premise at an answer cast makes both false. An existential without a bound, as in Capless, charges the body's use of the unpacked variable to the enclosing scope (A). A lambda containing such an unpacking cannot close, and at top level the unpacking puts $\topc$ in the use set, which makes \lean{dot\_effect\_safety} vacuous through the premise of \lean{Platform.root\_iff}. The existential now has a declared bound $\exc{\kappa}{C_0}{T}$. One cost remains. \lean{ELeCo.pack} has no side condition on its witness, so a local $\cany$ may flow into a result $\cfresh$, which the reference documentation forbids. Last, \lean{Platform.root\_iff} stated letter for letter is false once $\topc$ is in the set (MC, stage B0), so \treeCC adds the premise $\topc \notin C$.

\subsection{Classifiers}

A filter applied inside resolution is unsound (A). The outer expansion reopens every leaf that the filter admitted, and four weakening theorems become false. The counterexample was recomputed in a Lean scratch file. The projection is now data on the atom, $\restr{c}{\varphi}$, and the second stage of resolution consumes it. The rule $\ev{kcls}$ was first read at star binders too, with a star binder taken to have the top classifier. Stage K1 refuted that (MC). Renaming by an instance binder carries a star binder to an instance of an arbitrary capture set, so a star binder kinded at $\kexcept{\mathit{ThreadLocal}}$ is carried to a capability of that classifier, where the canonical form for kinding is false. The rule now fires at classified binders only. The price is that kinding is not complete at a star binder, as the second example below shows. A kinding morphism that took a closed chain and no hole was refuted at stage K2 (MC), and \lean{kindCle} carries the hole. The syntactic hypothesis of \lean{dot\_effect\_safety} was false over a projecting source. With a platform binder of classifier \lean{Control} and the use set $\cset{\restr{\kappa}{\konly{\mathit{Control}}}}$, the translated set does not contain $\kappa$ while $\kappa$ is a leaf. These are two lemma-level facts, and no complete program is exhibited. \treeCL takes the semantic hypothesis that $\kappa$ is not a leaf.

\subsection{Theorems that said nothing}

In October 2026 an audit asked of each theorem whether its hypotheses could hold and whether its conclusion said anything. PR~\#58 repaired what it found. The findings are evidence about what the trees prove, and they explain the form of the level theorems of Section~\ref{sec:levels}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lean{no\_inner\_escape} and \lean{lvl\_safety} were stated over a typed store. A typed store has no scope binder (\lean{Store.Typed.rootFree}), so every atom is at the outermost level and the hypothesis that a binder is not at or outside a scope capability could not hold. Both are now stated for member-free evidence with no store, and \lean{lvl\_safety} is proved from \lean{level\_inversion}.
\item \lean{lvl\_canon} had a conclusion that held for every capture set over a store. It is deleted from \treeCC and restated in \treeCL.
\item \lean{X3\_no\_escape} was an instance over a store that types \lean{X2Ctx}. No such store exists (\lean{X3\_no\_store}, by hand). \lean{E3\_capkI\_mor\_canon} had the same fault (\lean{E3CtxB\_no\_store}).
\item \lean{subclass\_\allowbreak or\_\allowbreak disjoint} was a tautology of the definition of \lean{disjointB}. \lean{compile\_\allowbreak rejected\_\allowbreak goal} had as its conclusion the certificate that its hypothesis contained. Both are restated with content.
\item \lean{E1\_effect\_safety} and \lean{E2\_effect\_safety} hold because the run inspects only closures annotated $\cempty$. The instances \lean{E1r\_read\_has\_root} and \lean{E2r\_read\_has\_root} were added so that the filter is exercised.
\end{itemize}
The level theorems of \treeCC are stated for member-free evidence because of example C3. Bad capture bounds are derivable under a lambda by $\ev{capvar}$, which reads them, and a level theorem proved by induction on all evidence fails at that case.

\begin{example}[C3, decided]
In \treeCC the body of the lambda of the term C3 has a context in which evidence puts a star binder below $\cempty$ (\lean{C3\_badBounds}). \lean{C3\_typed} (\lean{FCdot/Examples.lean:912}) is closed by \lean{checkTm\_sound} and \lean{decide +kernel}. \lean{C3\_no\_store} shows that over a typed store no evidence puts a star binder below $\cempty$, so no store realizes the context.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The price of one refusal, by hand]
In \treeCL the context \lean{K6Ctx} has a classified binder and a star binder. \lean{K6x\_kindLe} (\lean{FCdot/Examples.lean:3613}) says that the leaf of the star binder is admitted by $\kexcept{\mathit{ThreadLocal}}$. \lean{K6x\_no\_kindCo} (\lean{:3675}, by hand) says that no kinding evidence derives that fact. The proof renames by an instance binder and applies \lean{kind\_canon}.
\end{example}

\subsection{Separation}

A fourth tree, \lean{Separation}, was started as a copy of \treeCC, with a store invariant, ownership derived from heirs and consumption. Six rounds of review followed, and five refuted a decision. The work is parked on its own branch, \lean{separation}, off the default build targets. According to its commit message the modules through \lean{FormAlgebra} and \lean{Retyping} build and \lean{CanonicalForms} and \lean{Preservation} do not. Two machine-checked findings are open. The lemma \lean{substAtom\_store} is false under the design's decision 39, and an argument escapes through a closure that the callee returns. Three repairs are recorded and none was taken. The status is parked and not refuted, and no theorem of this report depends on the branch.
