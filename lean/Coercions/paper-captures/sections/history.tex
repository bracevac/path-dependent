\section{Designs That Did Not Work}\label{sec:history}
\mathplain

Several parts of the three trees look arbitrary until one sees what they replaced. This section records the designs that were dropped and the theorems that were found to say nothing. The kind of evidence against each design is given in parentheses. MC is a machine-checked counterexample, a Lean file that the kernel accepted. A is an argument on paper, usually by a reviewer who attacked the design before it was built. C is a cost judgment: the design would work and was refused for its price. A refusal of kind C, or with no kind, is a decision and not a refutation. The counterexample files and the design notes are in the author's archive of 2026-10-16, not in the repository.

\subsection{Where capture sets live}

Option (a) puts capture binders and an existential type in the target, as Capless has them. Option (b) puts the capture set of an object under its self binder, as a proposition $\plec{\cset{\self}}{C}$ of its telescope. Option (c) keeps the capture set outside the shape, as $\capt{S}{C}$, reads capture quantification as a capture member of an object, and reads a result $\cany$ as a closed set. Option (c) won. Under option (a) no rule fires on an unbounded capture binder at the caller, so a call whose result type is existential has no avoidance evidence (A). Option (a) also needs a second family of normal forms for answers (C). Under option (b) a closed side of a capture template cannot bridge two sets that mention the self. Templates become chains of hole indices, and the change reaches the checker, the normalizer and the composition of entries (C). A reviewer of option (c) as first written found that it typed the sides of a capture template in report~1's mode at the root of an atom, while report~1 types a side only in the plain mode (A). \treeCC later took option (a) for $\cfresh$ alone (Section~\ref{sec:levels-fresh}).

\subsection{Boxes three times}

The first design made a box a capture name on a label. A container holding $\dbox{f}$ is pure in the source, but the target's field condition forces the literal's annotation to contain the root of $f$, so the translation is not typed (A). A capture name hides nothing, since a witness at a label resolves through the store. Inertness belongs to the shape, hence the box shape $\boxt{T}$. The second design made a box an atom wrapper (MC). A type witness may mention the self inside a capture set. The evidence $\ev{member}$ at an atom that unboxes a box read $\cempty$ for the self, resolution at the root read $\cset{x}$, and canonical forms of closed equality evidence failed on a typed store. A box is now a value, stored like any literal, and an unboxing is a term. The third design erased a box to a one-field runtime object at a reserved label (MC). A source literal with one term member and the box of that member then erase alike, and so do a projection and an unboxing. Erasure alone ties the states of the simulation, and one steps while the other is stuck. The runtime now has an inert box, \lean{Tm.box}.

\subsection{The parameter \texorpdfstring{$\cany$}{any} and \texorpdfstring{$\cany$}{any} under a box, the DOT way}

\treeK reads no parameter $\cany$. It would be a capture member of a parameter object, allocated at each call. The target would then have an application per call that the source lacks, and erasure equality would be argued again at every call site (C and A). A program writes an explicit capture member instead. \treeK also reads no $\cany$ under a box, in a type-member bound or in the lower bound of a capture member. That refusal is a deferral, with no counterexample. Neither refusal is in the typing judgment. \lean{HasTy} does not mention the predicate \lean{AnyOk}, and the front end rejects the programs that fail it. \treeCC reads a parameter $\cany$ as the capture binder of the arrow and keeps the second refusal, so its \lean{withFile} example is monomorphic.

\subsection{The compiler's way}

The universal capability was first a binder at the head of the platform prefix, and two write-ups of the levels design disagreed about it (A). It is now an atom $\topc$, the compiler's constant, which leaves the platform prefix unchanged (C). An atom that absorbs every level types the escape at top level (A), so $\topc$ has the strictest level. Inserting a scope binder before another binder changes the level of the other and falsifies level evidence about it (A). So typed renamings and substitutions now keep scope binders and level facts. The substitution lemma \lean{Subst.Typed.singleC} then failed for an instance binder instantiated by $\topc$ (MC), and it now assumes that the binder is not an instance binder. The first design of existential answers let atoms and values carry answer casts. Preservation then failed at allocation, which drops the answer cast on a value, and progress failed at a packed atom with a plain answer (A). Now atoms and values carry no answer cast. Packing is an answer inclusion, \rn{Ans-Pack}, and the pack wrapper on atoms and values (\rn{T-PackA}, \rn{T-PackV}) stands only at an existential answer. Answer evidence with its own normal forms failed (MC), because two lemmas about cast redexes have no typing hypothesis. An existential without a bound, as in Capless, charges the body's use of the unpacked variable to the enclosing scope (A). Then a lambda containing such an unpacking cannot close, and an unpacking at top level puts $\topc$ in the use set and makes \lean{dot\_effect\_safety} vacuous. The existential now has a declared bound, $\exc{\kappa}{C_0}{T}$. \rn{Ans-Pack} still puts no level condition on its witness, so a local $\cany$ may flow into a result $\cfresh$, which the reference documentation forbids. Last, \lean{Platform.root\_iff} stated letter for letter is false once $\topc$ is in the set (MC), and \treeCC adds the premise $\topc \notin C$.

\subsection{Classifiers}

A filter applied inside resolution is unsound (A, recomputed in a Lean scratch file). The outer expansion reopens every leaf that the filter admitted, and four weakening theorems become false. The projection is now data on the atom, $\restr{c}{\chi}$, which resolution carries along and expansion applies last. \rn{Kd-Cls} was first read at star binders too, which were taken to have the top classifier (MC). Renaming by an instance binder can carry a star binder kinded at $\kexcept{\mathit{ThreadLocal}}$ to a capability of that classifier, where Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-kind} then fails. The rule now fires at classified binders only, and kinding is not complete at a star binder (Example~\ref{ex:cl-k6}). A kinding morphism with a closed chain and no hole failed too (MC), so \lean{kindCle} carries a hole. Last, \lean{dot\_effect\_safety} first assumed that $\kappa$ is not an atom of the translated use set, which a filtered set defeats (Example~\ref{ex:cl-filter}). In the program E1r of Example~\ref{ex:cl-try}, $\kappa_{\mathit{ctl}}$ is no atom of the translated use set and is a leaf of the inspected variable (\lean{E1r\_reads\_root}, by hand). So the old statement fails at a complete program, though no Lean statement says so (A). \treeCL assumes that $\kappa$ is not a leaf (Section~\ref{sec:source-thms}).

\subsection{Theorems that said nothing}

In October 2026 an audit asked of each theorem whether its hypotheses could hold and whether its conclusion said anything. PR~\#58 recorded the findings in the READMEs and comments, and one later commit per tree repaired the theorems (\texttt{1a24147}, \texttt{eb72b8c} and \texttt{72e4789}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \lean{no\_inner\_escape} and \lean{lvl\_safety} were stated over a typed store. A typed store has no scope binder (\lean{Store.Typed.rootFree}), so every atom is at the outermost level. The hypotheses of \lean{no\_inner\_escape} could not hold together, and the conclusion of \lean{lvl\_safety} followed from the store alone. Both are now corollaries of Theorem~\ref{thm:lvl-inv}, for member-free evidence with no store.
\item \lean{lvl\_canon} had a conclusion that held for every capture set over a store. It is deleted from \treeCC and restated in \treeCL for member-free evidence with no store.
\item Two examples assumed a store for a context that no store types, and two theorems were tautologies. All four are restated with content. \lean{compile\_rejected\_goal} remains only in \treeCL, and \lean{certify?\_sound} takes its place in \treeCC.
\item The runs of \lean{E1\_effect\_safety} and \lean{E2\_effect\_safety} in \treeCL inspect only closures annotated $\cempty$. \lean{E1r\_read\_has\_root} and \lean{E2r\_read\_has\_root} exercise the filter.
\end{itemize}
