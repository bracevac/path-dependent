\treeK reads $\cany$ by its position, the DOT way (Section~\ref{sec:source-any}). The Scala~3 compiler reads it by scope, as Section~\ref{sec:cc-later} described. A capability may flow into the local $\cany$ of its own scope or of a nested scope, never into that of an enclosing one~\cite{scala3cc}. \treeCC models this reading. Every theorem here is in \treeCC, and its head says whether \treeCL has it too.

\subsection{The program, and \texorpdfstring{$\mathit{c1}$}{c1} in \treeCC}\label{sec:levels-program}

Section~\ref{sec:cc-later} quoted the escape of \lstinline{withFile}, where the callback returns a closure that captures its file \lstinline{f}. The reference explains the error by its rule that result $\cfresh$ capabilities are isolated (\texttt{scoped-capabilities.md}, lines 396 to 412). Hiding \lstinline{f} needs a result $\cfresh$, which may not flow into the $\cany$ of the expected type, bound outside \lstinline{withFile}. In \treeCC the same program meets a level check. The file \lstinline{f} belongs to the nested scope of the callback and may not flow into that $\cany$. The promise is that the file does not outlive the callback. What is proved is narrower, and Section~\ref{sec:levels-open} says how.

\treeCC transcribes the running example of Section~\ref{sec:mini}, from \lean{capVal} and \lean{C1Ctx} on, and decides the typings of $\mathit{c1}$ and of both programs. The run of $\mathit{prog}_1$ takes the seven steps of Table~\ref{tab:mini-run} (\lean{C6\_run}, by hand). A call now also sends the body's scope binder to the universal capability (Section~\ref{sec:levels-run}), but nothing in $\mathit{c1}$ mentions one, so each step does what it did in \treeK. Effect safety for $\mathit{prog}_2$ is again the general theorem (\lean{C6\_safe}). Scopes change what $\cany$ means, not how a program without $\cany$ runs.

Figures~\ref{fig:cc} and~\ref{fig:cc-machine} give what \treeCC adds. A capture set may contain the universal capability $\topc$, the compiler's top-level $\cany$ read as a constant. A capture binder $\kappa : B$ has one of the four bounds of Figure~\ref{fig:target-syntax}, and \treeCC gives each a meaning. A scope binder $\varrho : \scopeb$ is the local $\cany$ of a scope. A star binder $\kappa : \cstar$ stands for itself, as in \treeK. An instance binder $\kappa \instb{C}$ stands for $C$. No rule constructs the fourth form, $\kappa : \mathsf{upper}\,C$. Scope and star binders are opaque. Four contexts open a scope:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}lcl@{}}
\mathit{scope}(\Gamma) & = & \Gamma, \varrho : \scopeb, \kappa : \cstar \\
\mathit{scope}(\Gamma, C) & = & \Gamma, \varrho : \scopeb, \kappa \instb{C} \\
\mathit{body}(\Gamma, T) & = & \Gamma, \varrho : \scopeb, \kappa : \cstar, x : T \\
\mathit{obj}(\Gamma, T) & = & \Gamma, \varrho : \scopeb, \self : T \mathrel{:=} \langle W, W^{c}, \dom F\rangle \qquad \text{for a literal } \litc{A}{W}{W^{c}}{F}
\end{array}
\]
In $\mathit{body}(\Gamma, T)$, $\varrho$ is the body's local $\cany$ and $x$ is the parameter. The arrow's capture binder $\kappa$ is what a parameter written $\cany$ reads as. A function shape $\pit[\kappa, x]{T}{E}$ binds $\kappa$ in $T$ and $E$ and binds $x$ in $E$. A lambda $\lamcc{A}{\varrho}{\kappa, x}{T}{t}{g}$ binds all three in $t$ and $g$. In $\mathit{obj}(\Gamma, T)$, $\varrho$ is the local $\cany$ of a class, bound before the self. $\mathit{scope}(\Gamma, C)$ is the scope a pack opens.

The level of a scope binder is itself. The level of any other binder is the innermost scope binder to its left, and $\topc$, the outermost level, if there is none. A scope capability is $\topc$ or a scope binder. The function $\leavesn{\Gamma}{n}{C}$ of Figure~\ref{fig:target-capev} is renamed $\capsr{\Gamma}{n}{C}$ here and called resolution, with the clauses for $\topc$ and capture binders below. Leaves now apply expansion after it. Expansion opens each scope capability into $\topc$ and every opaque binder at or outside its level:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}lcl@{}}
\lvl{\Gamma}{\topc} & = & \topc \\
\lvl{\Gamma}{\varrho} & = & \varrho \qquad \text{if } \Gamma \text{ binds } \varrho : \scopeb \\
\lvl{\Gamma}{y} & = & \text{the innermost scope binder left of } y\text{, else } \topc \qquad \text{for any other binder } y \\
\lvl{\Gamma}{\sel{x}{\ell}} & = & \lvl{\Gamma}{x} \\
\lvlle{\Gamma}{c}{r} & \text{iff} & \lvl{\Gamma}{c} = \topc\text{, or } r \text{ is a capture binder and } \lvl{\Gamma}{c} \text{ is } r \text{ or left of } r \\
\lvlle{\Gamma}{C}{r} & \text{iff} & \lvlle{\Gamma}{c}{r} \text{ for every } c \in C \\
\capsr{\Gamma}{n}{\topc} & = & \cset{\topc} \\
\capsr{\Gamma}{n}{\kappa} & = & \cset{\kappa} \qquad \text{if } \Gamma \text{ binds } \kappa : \scopeb \text{ or } \kappa : \cstar \\
\capsr{\Gamma}{n}{\kappa} & = & \capsr{\Gamma_0}{n}{C} \qquad \text{if } \Gamma = \Gamma_0, \kappa \instb{C}, \Gamma_1 \text{ or } \Gamma = \Gamma_0, \kappa : \mathsf{upper}\,C, \Gamma_1 \\
\expand{\Gamma}{r} & = & \cset{\topc} \cup \{\kappa \mid \kappa \text{ is opaque in } \Gamma \text{ and } \lvlle{\Gamma}{\kappa}{r}\} \qquad \text{if } r \text{ is a scope capability} \\
\expand{\Gamma}{c} & = & \cset{c} \qquad \text{for any other atom } c \\
\expand{\Gamma}{C} & = & \textstyle\bigcup_{c \in C} \expand{\Gamma}{c} \\
\leavesn{\Gamma}{n}{C} & = & \expand{\Gamma}{\capsr{\Gamma}{n}{C}}
\end{array}
\]
We read $\lvlle{\Gamma}{c}{r}$ as ``$c$ is at or outside the level of $r$''. The Lean's comment puts it as ``inner absorbs outer, never the reverse''. In $\mathit{body}(\Gamma, T)$ the parameter and the arrow's binder both have level $\varrho$ (\lean{scope\_order}, by \lean{rfl}). So a set that contains $\varrho$ has among its leaves $\topc$, $\varrho$, $\kappa$ and every opaque binder outside the body.

Capture prediction and effect safety, Theorems~\ref{thm:mini-pred} and~\ref{thm:mini-effect} in full, have the same Lean text in \treeCC as in \treeK, and their meaning changes with the leaves. Over a store a use set that contains $\topc$ covers every capability of the platform. Over a context with no scope binder, for a set whose resolution avoids $\topc$, expansion changes nothing and the leaves are those of \treeK (\lean{Ctx.roots\_eq\_caps\_of\_rootFree}). \lean{C6\_safe} computes the leaves of the use set of $\mathit{prog}_2$ by this fact.

\subsection{The level rule and the changed rules}\label{sec:levels-rule}

Capture inclusion gains \rn{C-Level}. Both of its sides are singletons, and a larger set goes below $\cset{r}$ by $\ev{union}$. The rule lets an outer capability flow into an inner scope. In the context $\kappa_1 : \cstar, \varrho : \scopeb, \kappa_2 : \cstar$ it derives $\cset{\kappa_1} \le \cset{\varrho}$ and $\cset{\topc} \le \cset{\varrho}$. Its premise fails for $\varrho$ and for $\kappa_2$ against $\topc$, and no member-free evidence puts either of them below $\cset{\topc}$ (\lean{X2\_outer\_not\_inner}, by hand, every level premise decided). The rule needs a scope capability on its right, so it never puts a capability below the arrow's binder, a star binder. Capture equality gains \rn{CE-Inst}. An instance binder $\kappa \instb{C}$ gives $\cset{\kappa} \equiv C$, and $\ev{symm}$ and $\ev{eqToLe}$ give both inclusions.

Four rules change for scopes, \rn{Sh-Pi}, \rn{V-Lam}, \rn{V-Obj} and \rn{T-App}. A call checks its argument $b$ at the domain with $\kappa$ at $\rt{b}$, so a parameter's $\cany$ is instantiated at the argument itself, not at a fresh capability. The README of \treeCC lists this among five departures of the calculus from the compiler. \rn{T-Let} and \rn{K-Let} take a body at an answer. \rn{S-Cap} stores any capture binder but a scope binder, and store extension appends only instance and bounded binders.

The source of \treeCC has the same scopes, and the translation sends each source binder to its target counterpart. The source names no $\topc$, so its level rule has a scope binder on the right, and its left side is never $\cany$ or $\cfresh$. It also has an instance rule:
\[
\inferrule*[left=\rn{Sc-Level}]{\src{\Gamma \text{ binds } \varrho : \scopeb} \\ \src{\lvlle{\Gamma}{c}{\varrho}}}{\subcj{\Gamma}{\cset{c}}{\cset{\varrho}}}
\qquad\qquad
\inferrule*[left=\rn{Sc-Inst}]{\src{\Gamma \text{ binds } \kappa \instb{C}}}{\subcj{\Gamma}{C}{\cset{\kappa}}}
\]
A translated capture set never contains $\topc$ (\lean{CaptureSet.top\_not\_mem\_translate}). Theorem~\ref{thm:source-effect} uses that over a platform a leaf of $C$ is a member of $C$ (\lean{Platform.root\_iff}). In \treeCC that lemma assumes $\topc \notin C$, and this fact discharges it.

\rn{C-Level} says when one step is allowed. Section~\ref{sec:levels-inversion} says what follows for evidence that mixes it with every other rule.

\subsection{The escape in the calculus}\label{sec:levels-escape}

\begin{example}[The escape in the calculus]\label{ex:lvl-escape}
Read \lstinline{File} as $\top$ and put the call at the top level. The callback's body is typed in $\Gamma_{\!X4} = \mathit{body}(\cdot, \capt{\top}{\cset{\kappa_f}})$, the Lean's \lean{X4Ctx}. The inner closure uses $f$, so its annotation must cover $f$, and the expected type asks for that annotation to be below the universal capability outside the call. So typing the program needs evidence for $\cset{f} \le \cset{\topc}$. That step is an argument in the comments of the Lean, not a theorem. The binder order decides the levels:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{\qquad}l@{}}
\Gamma_{\!X4} = \varrho : \scopeb,\ \kappa_f : \cstar,\ f : \capt{\top}{\cset{\kappa_f}} & \lvl{\Gamma_{\!X4}}{\kappa_f} = \lvl{\Gamma_{\!X4}}{f} = \varrho \\
\Gamma_{\!X5} = \kappa_f : \cstar,\ f : \capt{\top}{\cset{\kappa_f}},\ \varrho : \scopeb & \lvl{\Gamma_{\!X5}}{\kappa_f} = \lvl{\Gamma_{\!X5}}{f} = \topc
\end{array}
\]
In $\Gamma_{\!X4}$ the level premise fails. Neither $f$ nor $\kappa_f$ is at or outside $\topc$, and with one more scope binder $\varrho_{\mathit{out}}$ outside the call, $f$ is not at or outside $\varrho_{\mathit{out}}$ either (\lean{X4\_no\_level}, decided). No evidence at all closes the gap:
\[
\neg\, \exists g.\ \clej{\Gamma_{\!X4}}{g}{\cset{f}}{\cset{\topc}}
\]
(\lean{X4\_no\_escape}, by hand). The proof is Theorem~\ref{thm:lvl-plain} and three facts. The context is plain (\lean{X4Ctx\_plain}, decided). The set $\cset{f}$ resolves to $\cset{\kappa_f}$ at every fuel (\lean{X4\_caps}, by hand). And $\cset{\topc}$ resolves to itself, which is at or outside $\topc$. So $\cset{\kappa_f}$ would be at or outside $\topc$, which is false. In the other binder order, $\Gamma_{\!X5}$, the level rule fires and $\ev{level}\,f\,\topc$ proves $\cset{f} \le \cset{\topc}$ (\lean{X5\_fires}, decided).
\end{example}

Example~\ref{ex:lvl-escape} is the picture at the level of the target. Its context is the translation of the body context of a source lambda $\src{\lambda(f : \capt{\top}{\cset{\cany}})}$ over the empty platform (on paper). No translated program poses its goal, since a translated capture set never contains $\topc$. A source program meets the escape at a capability of its platform.

\begin{example}[The escape at the source]\label{ex:lvl-w5}
Let $\src{\Gamma_{\!W2}}$ be the body context of a hand-made $\src{\mathit{process} : \dall{f}{\capt{\mathit{File}}{\cset{\cany}}}{\top}}$ over a platform with two capabilities $\src{\kappa_1}$ and $\src{\kappa_2}$, the Lean's \lean{W2BodyCtx}. No source derivation, member-free or not, proves $\subcj{\Gamma_{\!W2}}{\cset{f}}{\cset{\kappa_1}}$ (\lean{W5\_no\_escape}, by hand). This is the source counterpart of Example~\ref{ex:lvl-escape}, in one hand-made context and at one platform capability, $\src{\kappa_1}$, the one bound first. A top-level $\cany$ reads as the whole platform set $\src{\cset{\kappa_1, \kappa_2}}$, not as $\src{\cset{\kappa_1}}$, and the statement at $\src{\cset{\kappa_1, \kappa_2}}$ is not in the Lean. $\src{\Gamma_{\!W2}}$ declares its one variable at a plain shape and binds no instance binder, so every source derivation in it is member-free (\lean{Subcap.memberFree\_of\_plain}), and Theorem~\ref{thm:lvl-source} at $r = \topc$ ends as in Example~\ref{ex:lvl-escape}. In a context with one more scope binder $\src{\varrho_{\mathit{out}}}$ outside the call, the premise of \rn{Sc-Level} fails for $\src{f}$ and $\src{\kappa_f}$ at $\src{\varrho_{\mathit{out}}}$ (\lean{W5\_no\_level}, decided). In the other binder order $\src{f}$ is at the outermost level (\lean{W6\_lvl}, decided), so every scope binder absorbs it.
\end{example}

\subsection{Level inversion}\label{sec:levels-inversion}

Level inversion says what member-free evidence can do in any number of steps. It never moves a set outward. What it puts below a set at or outside $r$ resolves to capabilities at or outside $r$. Member-free evidence, atoms and source derivations are defined by
\[
\begin{array}{@{}lcl@{}}
\mfree{f} & \text{iff} & f \text{ uses neither } \ev{member} \text{ nor } \ev{eqToLe}\text{, and } \mfree{a} \text{ for each atom } a \text{ under a } \ev{capvar} \text{ in } f \\
\mfree{a} & \text{iff} & \mfree{f} \text{ for the capture evidence } f \text{ of every cast and recap in } a \\
\mfree{\src{d}} & \text{iff} & \src{d} \text{ uses none of } \rn{Sc-Inst}\text{, } \rn{Sc-Sel-Lower} \text{ and } \rn{Sc-Sel-Upper}
\end{array}
\]
The restriction is the lesson of the bad capture bounds of Example~\ref{ex:target-bad}. A lambda body may assume $\plec{\cset{\kappa}}{\cempty}$ through the object type of a binder, and only $\ev{member}$ and $\ev{eqToLe}$ read such an assumption.

\begin{theorem}[Level inversion, \lean{level\_inversion} and \lean{atom\_level\_inversion} in \treeCC and \treeCL]\label{thm:lvl-inv}
Let $\Gamma$ be a context and $r$ a capture atom.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\clej{\Gamma}{f}{C}{D}$, $\mfree{f}$, and $\lvlle{\Gamma}{\capsr{\Gamma}{m}{D}}{r}$ for every $m$, then $\lvlle{\Gamma}{\capsr{\Gamma}{n}{C}}{r}$ for every $n$.
\item If $\atomj{\Gamma}{a}{\capt{S}{C}}$, $\mfree{a}$, and $\lvlle{\Gamma}{\capsr{\Gamma}{m}{C}}{r}$ for every $m$, then $\lvlle{\Gamma}{\capsr{\Gamma}{n}{\cset{\rt{a}}}}{r}$ for every $n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

There is no store and no hypothesis that $r$ is a scope capability. The proof is a mutual induction on the two typings and on member-freeness, separate from that of Theorem~\ref{thm:target-canon}. The theorem holds in every context and has content only in one that opens a scope:

\begin{lemma}[A store opens no scope, \leanin{Store.Typed.rootFree}{\treeCC and \treeCL}, with \lean{Store.Typed.confined}]\label{lem:lvl-rootfree}
Let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$. Then $\Gamma$ binds no scope binder. If moreover $\topc \notin C$, then $\lvlle{\Gamma}{C}{\topc}$.
\end{lemma}

So over a store every set is at the outermost level. The Lean keeps the hypothesis $\topc \notin C$, and its proof does not use it. Theorem~\ref{thm:target-canon} reads a store and Theorem~\ref{thm:lvl-inv} reads none, so the two cover different contexts. In a plain context the member-free restriction can go, where
\[
\begin{array}{@{}lcl@{}}
\mathit{plain}(\Gamma) & \text{iff} & \Gamma \text{ binds no instance binder, and every term binder of } \Gamma \text{ is opaque} \\
 & & \text{at a type } \capt{S}{C} \text{ with } \mathit{plain}(S) \\
\mathit{plain}(\bot) & = & \text{false} \\
\mathit{plain}(\sel{x}{A}) & = & \text{false} \\
\mathit{plain}(\pit[\kappa, x]{T}{E}) & = & \text{true} \\
\mathit{plain}(\boxt{T}) & = & \text{true} \\
\mathit{plain}(\obj{\tel}) & = & \text{true iff every proposition of } \tel \text{ is a bound } \pbnd{S'} \text{ with } \mathit{plain}(S')
\end{array}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Level inversion in a plain context, \leanin{level\_inversion\_plain}{\treeCC}]\label{thm:lvl-plain}
Let $\mathit{plain}(\Gamma)$ and let $r$ be a capture atom. If $\clej{\Gamma}{f}{C}{D}$ and $\lvlle{\Gamma}{\capsr{\Gamma}{m}{D}}{r}$ for every $m$, then $\lvlle{\Gamma}{\capsr{\Gamma}{n}{C}}{r}$ for every $n$.
\end{theorem}

In a plain context $\ev{member}$ finds only bounds and capture equality is syntactic. \treeCL does not have this theorem. The next one is about leaves:

\begin{theorem}[Leaf inversion, \lean{root\_inversion} and \lean{atom\_root\_inversion} in \treeCC]\label{thm:root-inv}
Let $\Gamma$ be a context.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\clej{\Gamma}{f}{C}{D}$ and $\mfree{f}$, then $\caple{\Gamma}{C}{D}$.
\item If $\atomj{\Gamma}{a}{\capt{S}{C}}$ and $\mfree{a}$, then $\caple{\Gamma}{\cset{\rt{a}}}{C}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

It is Theorem~\ref{thm:target-canon} for member-free evidence with no store, and the nearest Lean statement to Theorem~\ref{thm:mini-cap}. It is in \treeCC, not in \treeK. It separates two star binders of one level, which Theorem~\ref{thm:lvl-inv} cannot.

\begin{theorem}[Level inversion at the source, \leanin{source\_lvl\_safety}{\treeCC and \treeCL}]\label{thm:lvl-source}
Let $\src{\Gamma}$ be a well-formed source context, $\src{d}$ a derivation of $\subcj{\Gamma}{C}{D}$ with $\mfree{\src{d}}$, and $r$ a target capture atom. If $\lvlle{\trans{\Gamma}}{\capsr{\trans{\Gamma}}{m}{\trans{D}}}{r}$ for every $m$, then $\lvlle{\trans{\Gamma}}{\capsr{\trans{\Gamma}}{n}{\trans{C}}}{r}$ for every $n$.
\end{theorem}

The proof applies Theorem~\ref{thm:lvl-inv} to the translation of $\src{d}$, which is typed (\lean{Subcap.translate\_typed}) and member-free (\lean{Subcap.translate\_memberFree}). The Lean also states two target corollaries of Theorem~\ref{thm:lvl-inv} with a scope capability on the right, \lean{lvl\_safety} and \lean{no\_inner\_escape} (\treeCC and \treeCL). Section~\ref{sec:history} tells how earlier versions of them, stated over a typed store, said nothing.

\subsection{Running a body}\label{sec:levels-run}

\rn{E-App} sends $\varrho$ to $\topc$, $\kappa$ to $\rt{b}$ and the parameter to $b$. A running program is the outermost scope, so the local $\cany$ of a body it enters becomes the universal one. This is sound because a store opens no scope:

\begin{lemma}[Entering a body, \leanin{Subst.Typed.enter}{\treeCC and \treeCL}]\label{lem:lvl-enter}
Let $\Gamma$ bind no scope binder, and let $\atomj{\Gamma}{b}{T[\kappa \mapsto \rt{b}]}$. Then $[\varrho \mapsto \topc, \kappa \mapsto \rt{b}, x \mapsto b]$ is a typed substitution from $\mathit{body}(\Gamma, T)$ to $\Gamma$.
\end{lemma}

A substitution $s$ is typed from $\Gamma$ to $\Gamma'$ when it meets seven conditions (\lean{Subst.Typed}):
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{\quad}l@{}}
\text{variables} & \atomj{\Gamma'}{s(x)}{\Gamma(x)[s]} \text{ for every term binder } x \text{ of } \Gamma \\
\text{transparency} & \rt{s(x)} \text{ is transparent in } \Gamma' \text{ at the type } \Gamma(x)[s] \text{ for every transparent } x \text{ of } \Gamma \\
\text{definitions} & \rt{s(x)} \text{ keeps the definitions, capture definitions and fields of } x\text{, under } s \\
\text{scopes} & s(r) \text{ is a scope capability of } \Gamma' \text{ for every scope capability } r \text{ of } \Gamma \\
\text{levels} & \lvlle{\Gamma'}{s(c)}{s(r)} \text{ whenever } \lvlle{\Gamma}{c}{r} \text{ and } r \text{ is a scope capability of } \Gamma \\
\text{innermost scope} & \lvlle{\Gamma'}{r'}{s(r)} \text{ for the innermost scope capabilities } r \text{ of } \Gamma \text{ and } r' \text{ of } \Gamma' \\
\text{instances} & \Gamma' \text{ binds } s(\kappa) \instb{C[s]} \text{ for every instance binder } \kappa \instb{C} \text{ of } \Gamma
\end{array}
\]
The substitution lemma then carries every judgment from $\mathit{body}(\Gamma, T)$ to $\Gamma$. The hypothesis gives the conditions on levels and on the innermost scope, since with no scope binder every atom is at the outermost level. The machine applies the lemma at the context of its store (Lemma~\ref{lem:lvl-rootfree}). An opaque binder appended to a store would enlarge the expansion of $\topc$, so store extension appends none, and the machine stores a witness as an instance binder.

The use-set half of preservation, Lemma~\ref{lem:target-step} in \treeCC, has 18 cases, against 12 in \treeK. Of the six new ones, \rn{E-CastEPush}, \rn{E-CastEVal}, \rn{E-CastEAtom} and \rn{E-LetEx} relate equal sets. \rn{E-Unpack} and \rn{E-UnpackV} read the frame's $g$, the wrapper's $g_0$, rule \rn{CE-Inst} at the new binder, and the body's charge $f$, each through Theorem~\ref{thm:target-canon}, which gains the cases \rn{C-Level}, reading no store, and \rn{CE-Inst}. The proof keeps its shape, and most of the new work is in the substitution lemma.

\subsection{Fresh results}\label{sec:levels-fresh}

A result type written with $\cfresh$ promises a new capability at each call. \treeCC reads it as an existential answer $\exc{\kappa}{C_0}{T}$: there is a $\kappa$ below $C_0$ such that the result has type $T$. Answers stand only as codomains of function shapes and as types of terms. Packing is an answer inclusion, rule \rn{Ans-Pack}, and also a wrapper on atoms and values, rules \rn{T-PackA} and \rn{T-PackV}, which unpacking matches. Values and atoms carry no answer cast, and no rule types a packed value at a plain type, so a packed value is never stored. The source has no pack term, and packing there is subsumption. The term $\letex{\kappa}{x}{t}{u}{U}{g}{f}$ opens a star binder $\kappa$ and the payload $x$, and its body's charge $f$ may name $\kappa$. At run time the unpacking steps store the witness as an instance binder $\kappa \instb{C}$, which \rn{CE-Inst} reads.

\begin{remark}[Existentials are isolated, \leanin{no\_ex\_le\_ty}{\treeCC and \treeCL}]\label{rem:lvl-ex}
For every $\Gamma$, $\eta$, $C_0$, $T$ and $T'$, there is no derivation of $\elej{\Gamma}{\eta}{\exc{\kappa}{C_0}{T}}{T'}$.
\end{remark}

It is immediate from the rules. No rule of answer inclusion has an existential on the left and a plain type on the right, and \rn{Ans-Trans} keeps an existential an existential (\lean{ex\_stays\_ex}). It is the counterpart of the reference's rule that result $\cfresh$ capabilities are isolated (Section~\ref{sec:levels-program}).

\begin{example}[Two calls of a fresh result]\label{ex:lvl-calls}
Over the platform $\kappa_1 : \cstar$, the closure $\mathit{freshCell} : \capt{(\pit[\kappa', u]{\Unit}{\exc{\kappa}{\cset{\kappa_1}}{\capt{\mathit{Cell}}{\cset{\kappa}}}})}{\cset{\kappa_1}}$ is typed (\lean{Y1\_freshCell}, decided). A caller with no scope binder calls it twice and unpacks each result (\lean{Y1\_caller}, decided). In the context that two uses of \rn{T-LetEx} build, no capture evidence puts either opened binder below the other, and none puts either cell below the other (\lean{two\_calls\_incomparable}, by hand, four conjuncts). The statement is about the typing context, whose opened binders are star binders, and its proof types that context by a store that no run produces. At run time the machine stores the witnesses as instance binders $\kappa_1' \instb{\cset{\kappa_1}}$ and $\kappa_2' \instb{\cset{\kappa_1}}$, and there \rn{CE-Inst} puts each below the other (on paper).
\end{example}

\subsection{What is not proved}\label{sec:levels-open}

No theorem of \treeCC says that the escape program of Section~\ref{sec:levels-program} has no derivation, in the source or in the target. \lean{X4\_no\_escape} answers one subcapturing question in one hand-made target context. \lean{W5\_no\_escape} answers one in one hand-made source context, at one platform capability. The step from ``the program types'' to ``$\cset{f} \le \cset{r}$ is derivable'' is the argument of Example~\ref{ex:lvl-escape}. Whole-program rejection is the verdict of the front end (Section~\ref{sec:fe-reject}), and its certificate rules out member-free derivations of one goal. The use after close of the reference is not modelled, since nothing closes a file. That \rn{C-Level} matches the compiler's own level check is said in comments only.
