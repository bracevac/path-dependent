\section{Front Ends}\label{sec:frontends}
\mathplain

Each of the three trees has a front end, a Lean library that takes a program in this report's notation to a derivation of its calculus, translates it and runs it. A front end proves a composition. Its theorems apply theorems of the calculus to the derivation it returns, and they add no fact about the calculus. Table~\ref{tab:pipeline} sums up.

Two programs frame the section. The first is the escape of Section~\ref{sec:levels} as the front end of \treeCC reads it (\lean{EscSrc}), where \lean{File} abbreviates $\mu(f.\,\{\mathit{read} : \capt{(\forall(u : \top)\,\top)}{\cset{f}}\})$:
\begin{lstlisting}[mathescape=true]
cc% λ(g : ⊤).
  let cb : ($\forall$(f : File ^ {any}) ($\forall$(u : ⊤) File ^ {any}) ^ {any}) ^ {}
         = λ(f : File ^ {any}). λ(u : ⊤). f
  in cb
\end{lstlisting}
The annotation of \lean{cb} reads the \lean{any} of its result at the scope binder of the body of $\lambda(g : \top)$, outside the callback's scope, and the front end rejects the program with a certificate (Section~\ref{sec:fe-reject}). The second program is C2 of Section~\ref{sec:source}, which the front end of \treeK types at the use set $\{k_2\}$ (\lean{C2\_type}, decided), where the hand derivation has $\{\kappa_1, \kappa_2\}$.

\subsection{The pipeline}\label{sec:fe-pipeline}

A program is a Lean term inside a quotation, \lean{cap\%}, \lean{cc\%} or \lean{clsProg\%}. A program of \treeCL starts with a header that declares the classifiers, the platform, and optionally a use set and a kind. Name resolution inserts a $\kw{let}$ for every operand that is not a variable. It leaves a partial term whose empty slots are the annotations the program did not write: lambda domains, self shapes and field types. The elaborator fills the slots from expected types, and the typer returns a derivation of \DOTMNF with its use set. The derivation is translated and checked, and a machine for \DOTMNF runs the program.

The typer is modelled on the Scala 3 compiler at commit \lean{4dae25087d} \cite{scala3cc}. It follows the subtype checker \lean{TypeComparer}, \lean{CaptureSet.subCaptures} for capture sets, the level test \lean{acceptsLevelOf} in \treeCC and \treeCL, \lean{TypeOps.avoid} for an unannotated $\kw{let}$, and \lean{adaptBoxed} for missing boxes, and it tries alternatives in the compiler's order. The Lean lists where it differs, twelve items for \treeCC. For example, the calculus has no rule that compares two recursive types, so a $\mu$ is opened only at a variable, and its level rule asks for a scope capability on the right, where the compiler compares owners by containment. No theorem relates the typer to scalac. The machine of \FCdot is also written as a function, \lean{fcStep?}, proved to agree with the step relation, but no compiled program runs on it.

\subsection{Soundness is the result type}\label{sec:fe-sound}

The function \lean{compile} returns a fill $a$ of the program and a record
\[
\langle \mathit{tm},\ U_c,\ T_c,\ d : \dtyuj{U_c}{\platctx}{t_c}{T_c},\ \mathit{skel}(\mathit{tm}) = \mathit{skel}(a) \rangle ,
\]
with the elaborated term $\mathit{tm}$, its term $t_c$ of \DOTMNF, its use set, its type and a derivation of the calculus. The skeleton is defined in Section~\ref{sec:fe-input}. Soundness of the typer is the type of its result, and needs no theorem about the typer.

The theorems marked ``record'' in Table~\ref{tab:pipeline} take the hypothesis $h$ that \lean{compile} returned the record, and no proof reads $h$. Each is a theorem of the calculus applied to $d$, so it holds for any record. For instance, \lean{compile\_effect\_safety} in \treeK and \treeCC is the tree's copy of Theorem~\ref{thm:source-effect} at $d$ and at a run from $t_c$, for a platform capability $\kappa$ that is not an element of the list $U_c$. In \treeCL it asks in addition that $U_c$ write no filter, since $\kappa$ is not an atom of $\cset{\restr{\kappa}{\varphi}}$ and may be its leaf, and it concludes $\insp{\mathit{stt}} = x$. The classified theorems of \treeCL take the kind as an argument of \lean{compileKinded} or \lean{compileFiltered}. The kind a header declares is resolved, and none of the entry points reads it.

\subsection{What speaks of the input program}\label{sec:fe-input}

The skeleton of an annotated term keeps its binding structure and its labels and forgets the rest:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}lcl@{\qquad}l@{}}
\mathit{skel}(x) & = & x \\
\mathit{skel}(\lambda(x : T).\,t) & = & \lambda x.\,\mathit{skel}(t) \\
\mathit{skel}(\nu(x : S.\,\mathit{ds})) & = & \nu(x.\,\mathit{skel}(\mathit{ds})) \\
\mathit{skel}(x\,y) & = & x\,y \\
\mathit{skel}(x.a) & = & x.a \\
\mathit{skel}(\klet x : T = t \kin u) & = & \mathit{skel}(u)[y/x] & \text{if } \mathit{skel}(t) = y \\
\mathit{skel}(\klet x : T = t \kin u) & = & \klet x = \mathit{skel}(t) \kin \mathit{skel}(u) & \text{otherwise} \\
\mathit{skel}(\square\,x) & = & x \\
\mathit{skel}(C \multimap x) & = & x \\
\mathit{skel}((t : T)) & = & \mathit{skel}(t) \\
\mathit{skel}(\{A = T\}) & = & \{A\} \\
\mathit{skel}(\{C = c\}) & = & \{C\} \\
\mathit{skel}(\{a = t\}) & = & \{a = \mathit{skel}(t)\} \\
\mathit{skel}(\mathit{ds} \wedge \mathit{ds}') & = & \mathit{skel}(\mathit{ds}) \wedge \mathit{skel}(\mathit{ds}')
\end{array}
\]
In \treeCC and \treeCL the skeleton also forgets capture binders, and it does not tell a $\kw{let}$ from an unpacking.

Eight theorems are about the functions of the pipeline and read their hypotheses. \lean{compile\_faithful} is the only one that relates the record to the written program. The program resolves to a partial term $p$, and the fill $a$ agrees with every slot $p$ writes. The elaborator's first candidate on $p$ has the term, use set and type of the record, $a$ is read off that term, and the term has the skeleton of $a$. So the record's term may differ from the program where the skeleton forgets, as where box inference inserts a box. \lean{compile\_faithful\_written} and \lean{compile\_full} treat a program that writes every slot, which compiles as the typer's synthesis on its resolution. \lean{compileE\_fills}, \lean{compileE\_skel} and \lean{compileE\_slot} say that the fill agrees with every written slot, that the record's term has the skeleton of the partial term, and that a rejection naming a slot comes only from a program with an empty slot.

{\sloppy Inference is complete at direct sites. The theorems \lean{elab\_complete\_direct} and \lean{compile\_complete\_direct} assume a canonical fill, in which every empty slot sits where the elaborator runs the typer's own clause on the fill, that the typer accepts the fill, and, in \treeK, that the fill can be read back off the typer's term. From some fuel on, the program then compiles as its fill does. Completeness fails elsewhere. In \lean{Mid}, a call argument is a lambda that the typer types at a formal of the callee that is not the dominant one. The written program compiles. With the lambda's domain erased, the elaborator checks the argument at the dominant formal, needs a middle type the program does not write, and rejects at every budget (\lean{Mid\_rejected}, decided, in all three trees). \lean{Mid} is the capture front ends' example. The other known failure, \lean{AscMu}, belongs to the front end of \Rone only.\par}

\subsection{Rejections and certificates}\label{sec:fe-reject}

A rejection is a statement about an algorithm. \lean{Alg} is an inductive judgment with one rule for each alternative of the typer's subtype checker. The goal solvers are complete for \lean{Alg} up to the recursion limit: if \lean{Alg} derives a goal and a run ends with fuel left, the run answers it (\lean{shape?\_complete} and its kin). A run that ends with fuel left and no answer has no \lean{Alg} derivation (the \lean{\_reject} theorems). \lean{Alg} is sound for the calculus: \lean{Alg.answer}, \lean{Alg.sound\_shape} and \lean{Alg.sound\_cap} in \treeK, \lean{Alg.sound} in \treeCC and \treeCL. No theorem says that \lean{Alg} is complete for the calculus, and it is not. The bad-bounds program E1 of \treeK has a hand-built derivation through the middle type $x.A$, which the program does not write. The typer rejects it with fuel left (\lean{E1\_verdict}, decided), and the goal it fails at has no \lean{Alg} derivation (\lean{E1\_not\_alg}, decided). The Lean's comments say that scalac rejects E1 too. No run of the compiler in the tree records it.

Completeness cannot say that some fuel suffices. All alternatives share one tank, so an alternative that never ends, tried first, exhausts every tank. A run that exhausts it reports the recursion limit, which is never a rejection by the rules (\lean{LPlet\_limit}, decided). In \treeCC and \treeCL a verdict is \lean{ok}, \lean{rejected} or \lean{unknown}, and only the typer's own reasons reject: an \lean{any} or \lean{fresh} where the notation does not read it, a level escape, an existential at the top.

A level escape is rejected with a certificate. When the typer fails at a goal $C <: D$, the function \lean{certify?} looks for an atom $r$, the universal capability first and then each atom of $D$, that confines $D$ at every depth of resolution, and for a depth below four at which $C$ is not confined to $r$. If it finds them, the contrapositive of Theorem~\ref{thm:lvl-source} is the certificate: no member-free derivation proves $C <: D$ in that context (\leanin{escape\_rejected\_at}{\treeCC}, \leanin{certify?\_isSome}{\treeCC}). That is all a certificate says. It speaks of the one goal and the one context the typer reached, it rules out member-free derivations only, and it says nothing about other derivations of the program. For the escape, the goal is $\{f\} <: \{\kappa_g\}$, with $\kappa_g$ the scope binder of the body of $\lambda(g : \top)$. \lean{Esc\_compile\_rejected} says, by \lean{rfl}, that \lean{compile} at the default budget returns this rejection with its certificate, \lean{Esc\_rejected'}, built by hand from decided facts. \lean{Esc\_rejected} says that the escape compiles at no budget. Below 31 units of fuel the verdict is \lean{unknown}, which we evaluated and no theorem states.

\subsection{Informative instances}\label{sec:fe-instances}

An instance of effect safety says something only if its run inspects a variable with a leaf. Three do, one per tree. \lean{C2gb\_never\_reads\_k1} (\treeK) is about C2 with the client at $b$ called, at use set $\{k_2\}$. Its run inspects the client, the object $b$, which entered $c$ through a parameter declared at $\{k_1, k_2\}$, and its member \lean{run}, at steps 14, 16 and 18 (decided), and the theorem applies at each. \lean{ReadK2\_never\_reads\_k1} (\treeCC) binds $a$ at $\{k_1\}$ and $f$ at $\{k_2\}$ and calls $f$, at use set $\{k_2\}$ (decided), and its run inspects $f$ at step 6. \lean{CE1b\_reads\_only\_control} (\treeCL) declares $b$ at \lean{\{ctl, io\}.only[Control]} and calls it at step 5. Each applies \lean{compile\_effect\_safety\_get} or its filtered twin to a premise the kernel decides. Two instances hold for want of a leaf, as their docstrings say: the run of \lean{C2\_never\_reads\_k1} inspects only $c$, a closed function, and that of \lean{CE1\_reads\_only\_control} only $f$, declared at $\{\}$. For box inference, \lean{C7scala\_type} (\treeK, decided) types C7 written with no box and no unboxing, and an example the kernel checks shows the elaborated term with a box in each field and an unboxing before the call.

\subsection{What is not proved}\label{sec:fe-open}

No theorem says that the typer or \lean{Alg} is complete for the calculus. No theorem gives let insertion or box inference a meaning, so beyond the skeleton nothing relates the elaborated term to the written program. The documentation says that the typer finds the least use set the rules allow, a design claim with no theorem. No compiled program runs on the machine of \FCdot, nothing checks the kind a header of \treeCL declares, and the safety theorems are about programs over a platform, not at an open context.
