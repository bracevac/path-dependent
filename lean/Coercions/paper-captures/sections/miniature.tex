\section{The Proof in Miniature}\label{sec:mini}
\mathtgt

This section shows the shape of the whole proof of capture prediction on a fragment of the \treeK target. The fragment has closures, lets and calls, and capture sets made of term variables and capture variables. It has no casts, no objects beyond a unit literal, no boxes and no capture members. It is small enough to state every definition and to run one program by hand.

The fragment is not mechanized on its own, and Section~\ref{sec:mech-mini} maps each of its statements to the nearest declaration and says where they differ. Its running example is the pair of Lean examples \lean{C1} and \lean{C6} of \treeK, and a status in this section refers to them. Section~\ref{sec:problem-ideas} named two ideas, and this section shows the first, use sets as data, in full.

Before the definitions, here is the running example and what the section proves about it.

\paragraph{The platform.}
Two capture variables, $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$, stand for two primitive capabilities, a log and a console (Section~\ref{sec:cc-types}). A program reaches each through a capability value, a closure that it calls. The capabilities $\mathit{log}$ and $\mathit{console}$ are identity closures on $\Unit$, each annotated with its capture variable:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}lcl@{}}
v_{\mathit{log}} & = & \lamc{\cset{\kappa_1}}{w}{\Unit}{w}{\ev{elem}\,\cset{w}\,\cset{\kappa_1, w}} \\
v_{\mathit{console}} & = & \lamc{\cset{\kappa_2}}{w}{\Unit}{w}{\ev{elem}\,\cset{w}\,\cset{\kappa_2, w}}
\end{array}
\]
They do nothing. A call of $v_{\mathit{log}}$ stands for writing to the log (Section~\ref{sec:cc-uses}). The annotation $\cset{\kappa_1}$ of $v_{\mathit{log}}$ says that calling it may use $\kappa_1$. The evidence in the brackets shows that its body uses nothing beyond the annotation and the parameter. Here $\ev{elem}\,C\,D$ proves $C \le D$ when every element of $C$ is in $D$.

\paragraph{A closure that returns a closure.}
The closure $\mathit{c1}$ of Section~\ref{sec:cc-closures} takes a unit, calls $\mathit{log}$, and returns a closure that calls $\mathit{console}$:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}lcl@{}}
v_{\mathit{inner}} & = & \lamc{\cset{\mathit{console}}}{z}{\Unit}{\mathit{console}\,z}{\ev{elem}\,\cset{\mathit{console}, z}\,\cset{\mathit{console}, z}} \\
v_{\mathit{c1}} & = & \lambda^{\cset{\mathit{log}}}(y : \Unit).\,(\letc{r}{\mathit{log}\,y}{v_{\mathit{inner}}}{\cempty}{\ev{elem}\,\cempty\,\cempty}) \\
& & \quad \ann{\ev{elem}\,\cset{\mathit{log}, y}\,\cset{\mathit{log}, y}}
\end{array}
\]
Values are named $v_{\ldots}$, so that a value and the location that stores it never share a name. The annotation $\cset{\mathit{log}}$ of $v_{\mathit{c1}}$ does not mention $\mathit{console}$, as Section~\ref{sec:cc-closures} explained. The let in its body declares in brackets that $v_{\mathit{inner}}$ uses nothing, with evidence $\ev{elem}\,\cempty\,\cempty$.

\paragraph{Two programs.}
The store $\sigma_0$ starts with $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$, which bring the capture variables into scope and hold no value. Then come the two capabilities, the literal $\unitv$ at $\mathit{unit}$, and $\mathit{c1}$. Two programs run from it:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}lcl@{}}
\sigma_0 & = & \kappa_1,\ \kappa_2,\ \mathit{log} = v_{\mathit{log}},\ \mathit{console} = v_{\mathit{console}},\ \mathit{unit} = \unitv,\ \mathit{c1} = v_{\mathit{c1}} \\
\mathit{prog}_1 & = & \letc{\mathit{inner}}{\mathit{c1}\,\mathit{unit}}{\mathit{inner}\,\mathit{unit}}{\cset{\mathit{console}, \mathit{log}}}{f_1} \\
\mathit{prog}_2 & = & \letc{\mathit{inner}}{\mathit{c1}\,\mathit{unit}}{\mathit{unit}}{\cempty}{\ev{capvar}\,\mathit{unit}}
\end{array}
\]
Both call $\mathit{c1}$ and bind the closure it returns to $\mathit{inner}$. The first program calls $\mathit{inner}$. The second drops it and returns $\mathit{unit}$. The two lets declare the use sets $\cset{\mathit{console}, \mathit{log}}$ and $\cempty$ of their bodies, with evidence that the bodies fit them. Example~\ref{ex:mini-typing} builds the evidence $f_1$. The first program declares $\mathit{log}$ too, which its body does not call, to show that a declared set may be larger than the body needs. Example~\ref{ex:mini-alloc} shows the step that forgets it.

\paragraph{The claims.}
The run of $\mathit{prog}_1$ reaches a state that calls $\mathit{console}$ (Table~\ref{tab:mini-run} in Section~\ref{sec:mini-machine}). That is allowed, because the initial use set of $\mathit{prog}_1$ has the root $\kappa_2$ (Example~\ref{ex:mini-typing}). The use set of $\mathit{prog}_2$ has the one root $\kappa_1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:mini-effect} says that no state reached from $\mathit{prog}_2$ inspects a variable with root $\kappa_2$.

\paragraph{Roadmap.}
Why does the claim hold? A run reaches $\kappa_2$ only by calling $\mathit{console}$, or a closure whose annotation reaches $\mathit{console}$, such as the one $\mathit{prog}_2$ binds to $\mathit{inner}$. The let of $\mathit{prog}_2$ declares the empty set for its body, and its evidence $\ev{capvar}\,\mathit{unit}$ proves $\cset{\mathit{unit}} \le \cempty$. Each step that changes the use set is paid for by one piece of such evidence. So the roots of the use set never grow, and $\kappa_2$ is not among them at the start. At a call, Lemma~\ref{lem:mini-store} replaces both inversions of the textbook route of Section~\ref{sec:problem-textbook}, since the store binds a closure at its own type. What is left is the closing evidence of that closure, and Theorem~\ref{thm:mini-cap}, the fragment's form of Idea~2, reads it as coverage. Figure~\ref{fig:mini-proof} shows how the results of this section depend on one another.

Figure~\ref{fig:mini-syntax} gives the fragment. A context binds both kinds of variable. A term binder $x : T$ gives a variable its type. A capture binder $\kappa : \cstar$ introduces a capture variable, and its bound $\cstar$ says that it stands for itself, with no set above it. No term binds a capture variable. In a run, capture binders come from the store.

A type $\capt{S}{C}$ is a shape $S$ with a capture set $C$. The shapes of the fragment are $\top$, the empty object type of \Rone, and the function shape $\pit[x]{T}{T'}$, which binds its parameter $x$ in the codomain $T'$. The domain and the codomain are types, so each carries its own capture set. $\Unit$ is $\top$ with the empty set, and its value $\unitv$ is the literal with no witnesses and no fields. $\CapT{\kappa}$ is the type of a capability, a function on $\Unit$ that may use $\kappa$.

\paragraph{A capturing type.}
The type of $\mathit{c1}$ is
\[
T_{\mathit{c1}} = \capt{(\pit[y]{\Unit}{\capt{(\pit[z]{\Unit}{\Unit})}{\cset{\mathit{console}}}})}{\cset{\mathit{log}}} .
\]
The two sets are read as in Section~\ref{sec:cc-closures}, and both name term variables. Why does the annotation name $\mathit{log}$ rather than the capture variable $\kappa_1$? Because $\mathit{log}$ is the capability that $\mathit{c1}$ calls, and what it stands for can be read off its type, $\CapT{\kappa_1}$. The two choices mean the same, since $\cset{\mathit{log}}$ and $\cset{\kappa_1}$ have the same roots, as the next paragraph defines. The contexts of the example are
\[
\Gamma_0 = \kappa_1 : \cstar,\ \kappa_2 : \cstar,\ \mathit{log} : \CapT{\kappa_1},\ \mathit{console} : \CapT{\kappa_2},\ \mathit{unit} : \Unit
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Gamma_1 = \Gamma_0,\ \mathit{c1} : T_{\mathit{c1}} .
\]
The value $v_{\mathit{c1}}$ is typed in $\Gamma_0$, and $\Gamma_1$ is the context of the store $\sigma_0$ (Section~\ref{sec:mini-machine}).

\paragraph{Roots.}
Figure~\ref{fig:mini-syntax} defines the roots of a capture set by four equations. The roots of a set are those of its capture atoms. A capture variable is its own root. A term variable has the roots of the capture set of its type, computed in the part of the context before its binder. So the atom $\mathit{unit}$, whose type has the empty capture set, adds nothing to a set (Section~\ref{sec:cc-types}). The capture set of a binder mentions only binders before it, so the roots of a set do not change when the context grows at its end.

We write $\caple{\Gamma}{C}{D}$ when every root of $C$ is a root of $D$, and read it as ``$C$ is covered by $D$ in $\Gamma$'', in the sense fixed in Section~\ref{sec:cc-types}. We write $\capeqv{\Gamma}{C}{D}$ when $C$ and $D$ have the same roots.

\subsection{Evidence, typing and use sets}\label{sec:mini-typing}

Capture evidence is a proof term for an inclusion of capture sets. The judgment $\clej{\Gamma}{f}{C}{D}$ says that $f$ proves $C \le D$ in $\Gamma$. Figure~\ref{fig:mini-typing} gives its five rules, named as in Section~\ref{sec:intro}, followed by the typing of terms and values. Unlike the source judgment of Section~\ref{sec:problem}, $\tmj{\Gamma}{t}{T}$ has no use set, since $\uses{t}$ computes it.

\rn{C-Refl} and \rn{C-Trans} make capture inclusion a preorder. \rn{C-Elem} is a syntactic inclusion, which a checker decides. \rn{C-Union} bounds a union by bounding each part. \rn{C-Var} is the documentation's rule that a capability is below the capture set of its type (Section~\ref{sec:cc-types}). No rule replaces a capture variable by another set, as \rn{C-Var} does for a term variable.

\paragraph{Typing.}
The term rules are report~1's, restricted to variables, with capture sets and their evidence added. \rn{T-App} applies a variable to a variable, and the result type instantiates the codomain at the argument. The fragment has no subsumption, so \rn{T-App} needs the argument at the parameter's type exactly. In the full calculus report~1's casts supply subsumption (Section~\ref{sec:recap}). Two rules carry capture evidence of their own, in the brackets that erasure removes.

\rn{V-Lam} types a closure $\lamc{A}{x}{T}{t}{g}$ at $\capt{(\pit[x]{T}{T'})}{A}$, so its annotation becomes the capture set of its type. The closing evidence $g$ proves $\uses{t} \le \weak{A} \cup \cset{x}$. What the body uses is covered by the annotation and the parameter. The parameter is paid for by the caller, since a call uses its argument.

\rn{T-Let} types $\letc{x}{t}{u}{U}{f}$. Its avoidance evidence $f$ proves $\uses{u} \le \weak{U}$ under the binder of $x$, and the declared set $U$ does not mention $x$. This is avoidance (Section~\ref{sec:cc-uses}). The body may use $x$, but only through what $U$ covers.

\paragraph{Use sets and inspection.}
Figure~\ref{fig:mini-typing} computes the use set of a term by structural recursion, because every set a binder declares is data. A value uses nothing, so creating a closure uses nothing. What the body of a closure uses is charged at each call, through the annotation of the closure (Section~\ref{sec:mini-step}). A let uses what its bound term uses and the set its body declares. In the fragment only a call inspects, and it inspects its function, $\insp{x\,y} = x$ (Section~\ref{sec:cc-uses}).

\begin{example}[Typing the example]\label{ex:mini-typing}
Each capability is typed with closing evidence that is a syntactic inclusion. The body $w$ of $v_{\mathit{log}}$ uses $\cset{w}$, and $\ev{elem}\,\cset{w}\,\cset{\kappa_1, w}$ proves $\cset{w} \le \cset{\kappa_1} \cup \cset{w}$. So $v_{\mathit{log}}$ has type $\CapT{\kappa_1}$, and in the same way $v_{\mathit{console}}$ has type $\CapT{\kappa_2}$.

The inner closure $v_{\mathit{inner}}$ is typed in $\Gamma_0, y : \Unit, r : \Unit$. Its body $\mathit{console}\,z$ has type $\Unit$ by \rn{T-App}. It uses $\cset{\mathit{console}, z}$, which $\ev{elem}$ puts below $\cset{\mathit{console}} \cup \cset{z}$. The body of $v_{\mathit{c1}}$ is a let. Its bound term $\mathit{log}\,y$ has type $\Unit$. Its body $v_{\mathit{inner}}$ is a value and uses nothing, so the let declares $\cempty$ with avoidance evidence $\ev{elem}\,\cempty\,\cempty$. The type of $v_{\mathit{inner}}$ does not mention $r$, so the let has that type. The let uses $\uses{\mathit{log}\,y} \cup \cempty = \cset{\mathit{log}, y}$, and the closing evidence of $v_{\mathit{c1}}$ puts that below $\cset{\mathit{log}} \cup \cset{y}$. Hence $\valj{\Gamma_0}{v_{\mathit{c1}}}{T_{\mathit{c1}}}$.

In $\Gamma_1$ the program $\mathit{prog}_1$ binds $\mathit{inner}$ at $\capt{(\pit[z]{\Unit}{\Unit})}{\cset{\mathit{console}}}$, the codomain of $T_{\mathit{c1}}$ at $\mathit{unit}$. Its body $\mathit{inner}\,\mathit{unit}$ uses $\cset{\mathit{inner}, \mathit{unit}}$, and its avoidance evidence is
\[
\begin{array}{@{}lcl@{}}
f_1 & = & \ev{union}\ (\ev{trans}\ (\ev{capvar}\,\mathit{inner})\ (\ev{elem}\,\cset{\mathit{console}}\,\cset{\mathit{console}, \mathit{log}})) \\
& & \phantom{\ev{union}}\ (\ev{trans}\ (\ev{capvar}\,\mathit{unit})\ (\ev{elem}\,\cempty\,\cset{\mathit{console}, \mathit{log}})) .
\end{array}
\]
It is built in steps. By \rn{C-Var}, $\ev{capvar}\,\mathit{inner}$ proves $\cset{\mathit{inner}} \le \cset{\mathit{console}}$, the capture set of the type of $\mathit{inner}$. By \rn{C-Elem} and \rn{C-Trans} the first half proves $\cset{\mathit{inner}} \le \cset{\mathit{console}, \mathit{log}}$. The type of $\mathit{unit}$ is $\Unit$, so $\ev{capvar}\,\mathit{unit}$ proves $\cset{\mathit{unit}} \le \cempty$, and the second half proves $\cset{\mathit{unit}} \le \cset{\mathit{console}, \mathit{log}}$. \rn{C-Union} joins the halves into $\cset{\mathit{inner}} \cup \cset{\mathit{unit}} \le \cset{\mathit{console}, \mathit{log}}$.

The program $\mathit{prog}_2$ drops $\mathit{inner}$. Its body $\mathit{unit}$ uses $\cset{\mathit{unit}}$, and $\ev{capvar}\,\mathit{unit}$ proves $\cset{\mathit{unit}} \le \cempty$. Both programs have type $\Unit$ in $\Gamma_1$, and
\[
\uses{\mathit{prog}_1} = \cset{\mathit{c1}, \mathit{unit}, \mathit{console}, \mathit{log}} ,
\qquad
\uses{\mathit{prog}_2} = \cset{\mathit{c1}, \mathit{unit}} .
\]
In $\Gamma_1$ these use sets have the roots $\cset{\kappa_1, \kappa_2}$ and $\cset{\kappa_1}$, so $\kappa_2$ is a root of the first and not of the second, as the overview claimed. Status: the typings and use sets are decided, the roots of the second use set are built by hand, and those of the first are on paper.
\end{example}

\subsection{What capture evidence means}\label{sec:mini-canon}

Report~1 read closed evidence by normalizing it to a head form. Capture evidence gets no normal form. What a piece of capture evidence says is a fact about roots, and that fact is all that the rest of the proof uses. The base case is a variable.

\begin{lemma}[Roots of a variable]\label{lem:mini-var}
For every $\Gamma$ and $x$ with $\Gamma(x) = \capt{S}{C}$, $\capeqv{\Gamma}{\cset{x}}{C}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Gamma = \Gamma', x : \capt{S}{C}, \Gamma''$. By the first three equations for roots, $\roots{\Gamma}{\cset{x}} = \roots{\Gamma'}{C}$. The set $C$ mentions only binders of $\Gamma'$, so $\roots{\Gamma}{C} = \roots{\Gamma'}{C}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Capture evidence means root inclusion]\label{thm:mini-cap}
For every $\Gamma$, if $\clej{\Gamma}{f}{C}{D}$ then $\caple{\Gamma}{C}{D}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By induction on the derivation, one case per rule.
\begin{itemize}
\item \rn{C-Refl}: $\subseteq$ is reflexive.
\item \rn{C-Trans}: the two induction hypotheses compose, since $\subseteq$ is transitive.
\item \rn{C-Elem}: the roots of a set are the union of the roots of its capture atoms, and every atom of $C$ is an atom of $D$.
\item \rn{C-Union}: $\roots{\Gamma}{C_1 \cup C_2} = \roots{\Gamma}{C_1} \cup \roots{\Gamma}{C_2}$ by the first two equations, and each part is covered by $D$ by an induction hypothesis.
\item \rn{C-Var}: Lemma~\ref{lem:mini-var}. \qedhere
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

In the fragment the evidence could be replaced by the side condition $\preccurlyeq$ itself, which a checker decides. Capture members break this. Under the binder of Section~\ref{sec:problem-bad} the source derives $\subcj{\Gamma}{\cset{\mathit{log}}}{\cempty}$, which is no inclusion of roots, and the translation must still type the body that uses it. So the target keeps the derivation as evidence, and Section~\ref{sec:target-canon} shows that closed evidence over a typed store still means $\preccurlyeq$.
