Coauthor report — Learning to make the proof explicit
24 October 2026

The central explanatory argument is that evidence is first checked under
assumptions and later interpreted after those assumptions have been discharged
by typed stored values. Translation constructs the evidence; substitution closes
it; interpretation supplies the facts needed by execution. The capture extension
reuses this architecture, interpreting evidence as root inclusion and obtaining
application prediction from a closure's substituted closing certificate.

I read the complete manuscript, including its figures, implementation discussion,
and solutions before editing. The initial reader received only an immutable
manuscript snapshot and the stipulated PL prerequisites. A separate reviewer had
access to Lean. I retained responsibility for the organization, revisions, and
whole-argument check; their reports supplied evidence and counterexamples to the
exposition, rather than a substitute for those decisions.

The main failures in the original exposition were transitions between levels,
not a lack of definitions. The original translation began with x:Exact, while
execution later used x:Lit through an interface cast. Both passages were locally
accurate, but the complete literal-introduction/opening translation was missing.
A reader could not infer exact wrapper placement, the complete target prefix,
or how the runtime example was an instance of that translation. The initial
reader independently identified this failure.

A parallel omission occurred in capture prediction. The text said that
substituting the argument supplied closed evidence, then immediately displayed
an inclusion about uses(t[u:=b]). It never stated the calculus-specific identity
connecting substitution of uses(t) with the uses of the substituted term. It
also showed only the contract of translateUses and hand-written certificates,
so a reader could not calculate the generated evidence from source premises.

The bad-bounds argument already supported the intended reconstruction. I kept
its central reasoning: an open assumption y:Bad supports a conditional proof of
top <= bottom, but typed-store canonical forms cannot be applied to that open
context. Any store-backed atom at Bad would supply contradictory closed
evidence. Context well-formedness is insufficient; store realization is the
essential hypothesis. The revision makes the surrounding closing calculation
concrete without replacing this successful argument.

Representative changes

1. Complete source translation and closing (original translation.tex:48–56 and
core.tex:320–331; revised tutorial/closed-program.tex, Section 5.1).

Before: "We leave them implicit to isolate the two subsumptions" and
"Here we reuse x for the allocated location at Lit".

After: "Use x,f for its source binders and reserve ell,z for the two locations
allocated during execution." The manuscript defines
  R(c) = foldSelf_ExactTel(unfoldSelf(c)),
shows the whole target including cast({A=W},expose) and both reads through R(x),
and supplies theta=[x:=cast(ell,expose),f:=z]. It then calculates the remaining
body and the final nested-cast application. The typed store and the earlier
client derivation are now connected by an explicit computation. R is a defined
abbreviation, not a suppressed constructor.

The new TutorialExamples.FullSource namespace checks the actual four-let source
derivation, exact target translation, target typing, erasure, and the root/view
properties of R. Existing normalization checks supply the concrete views.
A fully instantiated Step.appCastRefl transition shows how these facts enable
one real machine step, retaining the returned argument wrapper.

2. Closing evidence and use-set substitution (original captures.tex:138–153;
revised captures.tex, "Capture prediction for application").

Before: "Substituting an argument atom b gives closed evidence that can be
interpreted over the typed store."

After: the proof first obtains
  g[u:=b] : (uses(t))theta <=c (A-up union {u})theta,
then explicitly states
  uses(t[u:=b]) = (uses(t))theta,
  (A-up union {u})theta = A union {root(b)}.
Only then does it apply capture canonical forms and the separate stored-
annotation equality roots({h})=roots(A). The text says: "The inclusion comes
from g; the middle equality comes from the stored annotation."

The improvement is the separation of three different justifications: typed
substitution, syntactic endpoint equalities, and semantic interpretation.
A wrapped-unit argument demonstrates why the body retains the whole atom while
its capture-set substitution uses only the root.

3. Generated capture evidence (original figure capture-translation.tex and
bridge.tex:78; revised tutorial/generated-closing.tex).

Before: the figure named translate and translateUses but supplied only their
output contract; the operational certificate was chosen as elem(U,U).

After: the text specifies the source Var/Sub/App derivation, including distinct
type and use-subcapturing premises, and computes both wrapped operands and
  union(refl{log};elem({log},U), refl{u};elem({u},U)).
It explicitly distinguishes this generated syntax from the shorter hand-written
certificate used in the trace. The reader can now reconstruct evidence
construction, rather than only check evidence supplied by the author.

4. Whole-argument organization and the second reader's counterexample.

The object-extension catalogue moved after capture prediction. The main route
now runs from core translation to closing, canonical forms and safety, then to
the analogous capture argument. Advanced object features and further variants
follow that argument. Cross-references identify the complete bridge as Section

A new reader, with no access to the first review or Lean, reconstructed all
three worked chains with passage citations. It still found that the generated

bare-receiver proof. I treated that as a further revision requirement.
The added subsection states the client's domain and the stored lambda's domain,
uses typed canonical forms to justify argument adaptation, and proves the
bound using root(cast(b,d))=root(b) and uses(cast(t,c))=uses(t). It also distinguishes
the outer generated closure's certificate from log's certificate on the next
call, then adds the unchanged continuation's roots to obtain the state case.
The targeted follow-up reconstructed these additional steps without supplying

Remaining weaknesses and limits

The tutorial still requires active tracking of source recursive types versus
target self telescopes, the distinct evidence sorts, and root(a) versus roots(C).
The exact client calculation precedes the literal-origin version, so reading the
complete example still requires returning to earlier clauses. This is now
signposted and computationally connected, but remains a pedagogical cost.

The generated capture body and certificate are calculated from actual translation
clauses and independently checked against Lean by the fidelity reviewer; they
are not a new kernel-checked companion example. The simplified operational trace
is still distinct from that generated syntax. The prediction proof connects
them, but a complete numbered execution of every generated administrative step
is not printed or newly mechanized.

Recursive self-bounds, intersections, paths, scopes, and classifiers remain
survey material. The tutorial supplies the local reasoning needed for the
worked arguments, not the full proofs or unrestricted combinations of those
extensions. The capture examples model variable inspection, with conservative
identity closures standing in for named capabilities; they do not implement I/O.

The evidence for comprehensibility is a constrained model-reader reconstruction
with explicit citations, not a human usability study. It demonstrates that the
critical local bridges are supplied; it does not establish that every intended
reader will find the manuscript easy.
