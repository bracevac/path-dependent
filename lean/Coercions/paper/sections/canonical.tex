\section{Canonical Forms of Closed Evidence}\label{sec:canonical}

This section scales the normalization theorem of Section~\ref{sec:miniature} up to the full calculus. Over a typed store every binder is transparent, every block name is defined by a stored literal, and evidence has no assumptions beyond those definitions. The section shows that such evidence normalizes, and that its normal forms have the shapes that preservation and progress need. The structure is that of the miniature: resolution, forms, the normalizer, typed forms, a composition lemma, an application lemma, and the theorem. What is new is resolution in the presence of cycles, the forms that bounds need, and a second mode of typedness for recursive types.

\subsection{Shapes and resolution}\label{sec:resolution}

A head form promises a shape for its endpoints, which is $\bot$, $\top$, a function type, or an object type, and shapes are read after following the definitions of the store. Write $\Gamma(x) \ni \ell \mathrel{:=} W$ when $x$ is transparent in $\Gamma$ with witness $W$ at $\ell$. One step of resolution, $\mathit{next}_\Gamma(T)$, is $W$ when $T$ is a name $\sel{x}{\ell}$ with $\Gamma(x) \ni \ell \mathrel{:=} W$, and is undefined when $T$ is any other type or the name of an opaque binder. A chain of definitions may be cyclic, because a witness may itself be a name of the same block, as in the literal
\[
\lit{A = \sel{\self}{B},\ B = \sel{\self}{A}}{\tnil} .
\]
Resolution therefore runs with fuel:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}lcl@{}}
\resolven{\Gamma}{0}{T} & = & \top \text{ if } \mathit{next}_\Gamma(T) \text{ defined, else } T \\
\resolven{\Gamma}{n+1}{T} & = & \resolven{\Gamma}{n}{W} \text{ if } \mathit{next}_\Gamma(T) = W, \text{ else } T \\
\resolve{\Gamma}{T} & = & \resolven{\Gamma}{|\mathit{defs}(\Gamma)| + 1}{T}
\end{array}
\]
where $\mathit{defs}(\Gamma)$ lists the pairs of a transparent binder and a label its witness list mentions. A name at a label the list does not mention is defined as $\top$ and settles in one step. A chain that runs out of fuel on a defined name is cyclic, and a cyclic alias resolves to $\top$, the object type that asserts nothing. The only facts known about the names of a cycle are their equalities with each other, and $\top$ asserts nothing more. Resolving to $\bot$ instead would be unsound. A field of the literal above declared at $\sel{\self}{A}$ holds a value. An atom bound to that field has a type that resolves through the cycle. And item 5 of Theorem~\ref{thm:canon} says that the type of a well-typed atom never resolves to $\bot$. The fuel is enough. A chain of definitions either settles within $|\mathit{defs}(\Gamma)| + 1$ steps, at a shape or at an undefined name, or it visits a defined name twice and is periodic from then on. This is a pigeonhole argument on the finitely many defined names. It gives the two facts used everywhere below, with no side condition on the context.

\begin{lemma}[Resolution]\label{lem:resolve}
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\Gamma(x) \ni \ell \mathrel{:=} W$ then
\[ \resolve{\Gamma}{\sel{x}{\ell}} = \resolve{\Gamma}{W} . \]
\item For every $T$,
\[ \resolve{\Gamma}{\resolve{\Gamma}{T}} = \resolve{\Gamma}{T} . \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

The first version of the development required definitions to be well founded, with a side condition on literals that a witness may not be a bare name of the same block, the self-alias restriction. The condition propagated into the translation and, from there, into a restriction on the source rule \rn{\{\}-I}. Replacing well-founded resolution by the fuel-and-pigeonhole one removed the condition on both sides. Section~\ref{sec:history} returns to this.

Figure~\ref{fig:forms} gives the normal forms: the head form of an inclusion, the entries of an object form, and the view of an atom. A head form of an inclusion is $\form{bot}$, $\form{top}$, the identity $\form{id}$, a function coercion $\form{pi}\,d\,c$, or an object coercion $\form{obj}\,\mathit{Es}$ with one entry per proposition of the target telescope. The identity covers every inclusion between endpoints that resolve equally, so reflexivity and an inclusion read off closed equality evidence have the same normal form, and equality evidence needs no head form of its own. The domain and codomain evidence of a function form is not normalized further, because the machine uses it as a cast and normalizes it only when it is applied.

Entries come in three sorts: local entries, bound entries, and free entries. A local entry proves one target proposition from the propositions of the source telescope. The entry $\form{le}\,F\,H\,G$ is the normal form of a basic template: two sides, $\form{id}$ standing for an absent one, around a hole $H$ that still names a source proposition. The entry $\form{trans}\,F\,L_1\,L_2\,G$ is the normal form of a composition template: $L_1$ and $L_2$ are local entries for two inclusions that meet at a middle type, and $F$ and $G$ are its sides. A local entry is therefore a finite tree whose leaves are holes. Equality and presence entries name a source proposition by index, and $\form{copy}\,j$ copies the bound at position $j$ of the source. An entry of an object form is a local entry or a bound entry $\form{bnd}\,G$, a closed coercion out of the source object type as a whole. Free entries belong to the $\form{into}$ forms of the next paragraph.

Two head forms belong to self-bounds. The form $\form{bnd}\,i\,F$ casts through the $i$-th bound of the source object type and continues by $F$ from the type of that bound. The form $\form{into}\,\mathit{Ds}$ is a coercion into an object type whose entries never consult the view of the source. Its free entries are bound entries $\form{bnd}\,G$, closed coercions out of the source, and routed entries $\form{thru}\,R\,L$, in which the form $R$ reaches some object type from the source and the local entry $L$ proves the target proposition there. A route ends in a local entry, so routes never nest, and only $\form{into}$ forms carry them. A route is a subterm of the form that carries it, so applying a form to a view recurses on smaller forms.

\begin{example}[Why routes]
Let $z$ be a location whose literal defines $A$ as $\trans{\dfld{a}{T'}}$, and let $S = \obj{[\,\pbnd{\sel{z}{A}}\,]}$, the translation of $\src{z.A \wedge \top}$. A coercion from $S$ to $\trans{\dfld{a}{T}}$, for some evidence of $\trans{T'} \le \trans{T}$, goes through the bound to $\sel{z}{A}$, which resolves to $\trans{\dfld{a}{T'}}$, and then through the object coercion of the field rule. Its head form is $\form{bnd}\,0\,(\form{obj}\,\mathit{Es})$. Now pair it with a second coercion out of $S$, as the source rule \rn{And} does. The pair has to prove the field's propositions, and the only place they can be proven is the object type behind the bound. No proposition of $S$ itself says anything about $a$, so an $\form{obj}$ form, whose holes name propositions of $S$, cannot express the pair. A routed entry $\form{thru}\,(\form{bnd}\,0\,\form{id})\,L$ can: it reaches the object type behind the bound, and $L$, an entry of $\mathit{Es}$, proves the proposition there.
\end{example}

A view is the telescope of forms of the propositions that an atom's resolved object type asserts about it. The form of an inclusion proposition is a head form, an equality proposition records only that its two sides resolve equally, and a presence proposition records the binder and the field, $\form{has}\,x\,\ell$. A bound proposition records a form $\form{bnd}\,G$ from the type of the root into the bound, since a bound of an opened telescope is a fact about the root.

Figure~\ref{fig:normalizer} gives the normalizer, a family of mutually recursive functions indexed by fuel. Each returns an option, and the results are monotone in the fuel and deterministic, so the fuel is bookkeeping: a statement about a normal form holds for some fuel and for every larger one. Normalization of an inclusion is written $\hnf{\sigma}{e}{n}{F}$, the view of an atom $\vw{\sigma}{a}{n}{V}$, and the chain of casts of an atom $\chn{\sigma}{a}{n}{F}$. Presence evidence normalizes to the binder and the field it names. $\ev{field}\,\ell$ at $x$ gives $(x, \ell)$, and $\ev{member}\,a\,e\,i$ reads $\form{has}\,y\,\ell$ at position $i$ of the view of $a$ through the form of $e$ and gives $(y, \ell)$.

A basic template normalizes its sides and keeps its hole. A composition template normalizes its two premise morphisms, each of which proves a single inclusion and so has a single local entry, and keeps them as the two branches of a composition entry with identity sides. No branch is evaluated, because there is no view to evaluate it against yet. The precise view of a stored literal lists $\form{eq}$ for each witness and $\form{has}\,x\,\ell$ for each field, in the order of the literal's precise telescope. The view of a cast atom is the view of the underlying atom pushed through the head form of the cast, and folding, unfolding, and pairing act on views as they act on telescopes.

Composition, $F \circ G$ in Figure~\ref{fig:combine}, is where the design of Section~\ref{sec:why-structural} shows. Two function forms compose contravariantly in the domain and by substituting the domain cast for the parameter in the first codomain. Two object forms compose by substitution: every source fact that an entry of the second form names is replaced by the first form's entry for that fact. Consider a basic template $\form{le}\,F\,H\,G$ of the second form whose hole names the inclusion $j$ of the middle telescope. The first form's entry at $j$ is a local inclusion entry, basic or composite, and the substitution keeps it and composes its outer sides with $F$ and $G$. When the hole names an equality, the first form's entry at $j$ is an equality entry, and the hole is redirected to the source equality it names, flipped if the entry flips. A composition entry substitutes into both of its branches. Equality and presence entries follow the index, a copied bound takes the first form's entry at its position as it is, and a bound entry $\form{bnd}\,G$ becomes $\form{bnd}\,(\form{obj}\,\mathit{Es}_1 \circ G)$. Every recursive call is on a strict subterm, and no view is consulted. A composition entry records no middle type at all: its branches meet only when the entry is applied to a view, where each has become a form.

Prefixing an entry by a form $F$ makes it read the object type that $F$ reaches. A bound entry $\form{bnd}\,G$ becomes $\form{bnd}\,(F \circ G)$, a routed entry $\form{thru}\,R\,L$ becomes $\form{thru}\,(F \circ R)\,L$, and a local entry $L$ becomes $\form{thru}\,F\,L$. A copied bound is routed like every other local entry rather than composed, so that a later bound cast through its position still finds the bound it names, which is the last row of Figure~\ref{fig:combine}.

Application of an object form to an atom is pointwise. It is written $\mathit{entriesAt}_\sigma(a,\allowbreak C,\allowbreak V,\allowbreak \mathit{Es})$ and takes the view $V$ of the atom and the form $C$ of its chain of casts. A local entry is interpreted against $V$ alone. A basic template replaces its hole by the view's form at the named position, an equality reading as $\form{id}$, and composes the result with its sides. A composition template interprets its branches to forms $F_1$ and $F_2$ and returns $F \circ F_1 \circ F_2 \circ G$ for its sides $F$ and $G$. An equality entry requires the view to hold an equality at the named position, and a presence entry and a copied bound copy what they find there. A bound entry $\form{bnd}\,G$ is instantiated as $C \circ G$, which is why application takes the chain of casts and not only the view. Application of the free entries of an $\form{into}$ form, $\mathit{freeEntriesAt}$, instantiates a bound entry the same way, and a routed entry $\form{thru}\,R\,L$ by computing the view of the atom through $R$ and interpreting $L$ there.

Pairing produces an $\form{into}$ form. When either component is $\form{bot}$ the pair is $\form{bot}$, when both are $\form{top}$ it is $\form{top}$, and a component that is $\form{bot}$ behind bound casts is typed evidence for every inclusion out of its source and is returned as it is. Otherwise each component contributes free entries, and the pair concatenates them. An $\form{into}$ form contributes its own, a bound cast $\form{bnd}\,i\,F$ contributes those of $F$ prefixed by $\form{bnd}\,i\,\form{id}$, and an object form contributes its entries prefixed by $\form{id}$. The forms $\form{id}$ and $\form{top}$ count as object forms here, with the identity entries of the annotated telescope: $\form{le}\,\form{id}\,(\ev{le}\,j)\,\form{id}$, $\form{eq}\,j\,\mathit{false}$, $\form{has}\,j$, or $\form{copy}\,j$ at each position $j$, according to the kind of its proposition. A component may be a bound cast, whose target propositions are reached through an object type other than the source, and this is the case the routed entries exist for. An $\form{obj}$ form names source propositions by index and cannot express it.

The chain of casts of an atom, $\chn{\sigma}{a}{n}{F}$, composes the head forms of the casts on the atom from its root outwards, pairs at $\ev{both}$, and passes through $\ev{fold}$ and $\ev{unfold}$. Its result is a form out of the type of the root, and it is the form the machine consults at an application.

A head form is typed syntactically, in Figure~\ref{fig:formtyped}: it is typed evidence for $S \le T$ when its pieces of evidence are typed and the two endpoints resolve to the shapes the form promises. The entries of an object form are typed between two telescopes, entry by entry, against the morphism rules of Figure~\ref{fig:evidence}. A composition entry is typed by two local entries that meet at a middle type $M$. The sides of every template are closed, but $M$ lives under the self binder and may mention it. The domain and codomain of a function form are typed as evidence, since that is how the machine uses them. A view is typed against a telescope instantiated at the root of the atom.

Typedness has two modes, indexed by an optional root $\rho$, and keeping them apart is what makes the elimination case of the theorem go through. In the plain mode, written $\formj{\Gamma}{F}{S}{T}$, the shape of a type is its resolution, and a coercion form is typed between closed types unrelated to any particular atom. This is what lets the $\ev{member}$ case of the theorem extract a form from a view and reuse it as a coercion at no root at all. In the mode at a root $r$ the shape of a type is its resolution with the self block opened at that root:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}rcl@{}}
\resolveAt{\Gamma}{r}{T} & = & \mathit{open}_r(\resolve{\Gamma}{T}) \\
\mathit{open}_r(\obj{\tel}) & = & \obj{\weak{(\inst{\tel}{r})}} \qquad \mathit{open}_r(T) = T \text{ otherwise} .
\end{array}
\]
A telescope is open in a mode when opening leaves its object type unchanged, which every telescope is in the plain mode. The chain of casts of an atom is typed in the mode at its root, and opening makes $\ev{fold}$ and $\ev{unfold}$ invisible to it. The two modes are one inductive family, and every lemma about typed forms is proven once for both. The only rule that mixes them is the view rule for a bound: the form of a bound in the view of an atom is typed at the root, from the type of the root into the bound, because a bound of an opened telescope is a fact about the root. The first version of the development defined the rooted judgment as the plain one at opened endpoints. Bounds ended that, because the form that continues from a bound has the bound's type as its source, and that type has to be opened at the root as well.

\begin{example}[Why a second mode]
Let $x$ be a location of type $\obj{\tel}$, and consider the atom $\ev{fold}\,\tel\,(\ev{unfold}\,x)$. It has the type $\obj{\tel}$ again, and its chain of casts is $\form{id}$, since it has no casts. In between, $\ev{unfold}\,x$ has the type $\obj{\weak{(\inst{\tel}{x})}}$, which differs syntactically from $\obj{\tel}$ whenever $\tel$ mentions the self. Now cast the unfolded atom, $\ev{fold}\,\tel'\,(\ev{cast}\,(\ev{unfold}\,x)\,e)$. Its chain of casts is the form of $e$, which is typed from $\obj{\weak{(\inst{\tel}{x})}}$, the type of the unfolded atom, and not from $\Gamma(x) = \obj{\tel}$. In the plain mode the chain would not be typed from the type of the root. In the mode at $x$ both types open to $\obj{\weak{(\inst{\tel}{x})}}$, and the chain is typed from the type of the root to the type of the atom, which is what the machine needs at an application.
\end{example}

\subsection{Composition, pairing, and application preserve typedness}

\begin{lemma}[Composition]\label{lem:combine}
If, in one mode $\rho$,
\[ \chainj{\Gamma}{\rho}{F}{S}{M} \quad\text{and}\quad \chainj{\Gamma}{\rho}{G}{M}{T} \]
then $F \circ G$ is defined and $\chainj{\Gamma}{\rho}{F \circ G}{S}{T}$.
\end{lemma}

The proof is an induction on the sum of the sizes of the two forms, mutually with the analogous statements for substituting typed entries into typed entries, for composing the sides of a typed local entry with typed forms, and for prefixing typed entries by a typed form. Substitution asks the source and middle telescopes to be open in the mode, which holds for every object form that the lemma composes, because an object form is typed between resolved and opened types. The object case is the one that matters. A basic template of the second form names an inclusion of the middle telescope, the first form's typed entry there proves that inclusion from the source, and the sides compose by the induction hypothesis in the plain mode, because sides are closed. A composition entry substitutes into its two branches, which keep their middle type. Two function forms compose because the domain casts compose in the reverse direction and the substitution of a typed cast for the parameter preserves the typing of the codomain evidence. A bound cast composed with a form continues by the composite, and an object or $\form{into}$ form composed with a bound cast reads its entry at the bound, which is a strict subterm.

\begin{lemma}[Pairing and application]\label{lem:pair-apply}
Let $\tel_1$ and $\tel_2$ be open in the mode $\rho$, and let $\tel'_1$ and $\tel'_2$ have the kinds of propositions of $\tel_1$ and $\tel_2$, in the same order. If
\[ \chainj{\Gamma}{\rho}{F}{S}{\obj{\tel_1}} \quad\text{and}\quad \chainj{\Gamma}{\rho}{G}{S}{\obj{\tel_2}} \]
then $\mathit{pair}_{\tel'_1,\tel'_2}\,F\,G$ is defined and typed at $S \le \obj{\tel_1 \mathbin{+\!\!+} \tel_2}$ in the same mode. Let $a$ be an atom with root $r$. If
\[ \entjm{\Gamma}{r}{\mathit{Es}}{\tel_1}{\tel_2}, \qquad \viewj{\Gamma}{r}{\sigma}{V}{\tel_1}, \qquad \chainj{\Gamma}{r}{C}{\Gamma(r)}{S} \]
for a type $S$ with $\resolveAt{\Gamma}{r}{S} = \obj{\tel_1}$, then $\mathit{entriesAt}_\sigma(a, C, V, \mathit{Es})$ is defined and is a view typed at $\tel_2$.
\end{lemma}

The annotations of a pair need only agree with the target telescopes in their kinds, because they only supply identity entries. This matters at the root, where the chain of casts pairs at the annotated telescopes and is typed at their openings. The pairing lemma reads the entries of each component free of the source's view, which is possible because an $\form{obj}$ form's entries can be routed through the identity and a bound cast's entries through the bound.

\subsection{The theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical forms]\label{thm:canon}
Let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\lej{\Gamma}{e}{S}{T}$ then $\hnf{\sigma}{e}{n}{F}$ for some $n$ and $F$ with $\formj{\Gamma}{F}{S}{T}$.
\item If $\eqj{\Gamma}{\varphi}{S}{T}$ then $\resolve{\Gamma}{S} = \resolve{\Gamma}{T}$.
\item If $\hasj{\Gamma}{h}{x}{\ell}$ then $h$ normalizes to the pair $(x, \ell)$ and $\sigma(x)$ is a literal with a field $\ell$.
\item If $\morj{\Gamma}{m}{\tel}{\tel'}$ then the entries of $m$ normalize to $\mathit{Es}$ with $\entj{\Gamma}{\mathit{Es}}{\tel}{\tel'}$.
\item If $\atomj{\Gamma}{a}{S}$ then $\vw{\sigma}{a}{n}{V}$ for some $n$ and $V$, with $\resolve{\Gamma}{S} \neq \bot$, and
\[ \viewj{\Gamma}{\rt{a}}{\sigma}{V}{\tel} \]
whenever $\resolve{\Gamma}{S} = \obj{\tel}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By mutual structural induction on the evidence derivations, together with Theorem~\ref{thm:chain} below, because the view of an atom through a bound entry reads the chain of casts of the atom.
\begin{itemize}
\item The cases for $\ev{refl}$, $\ev{top}$, $\ev{bot}$, $\ev{eqToLe}$, and $\ev{pi}$ produce their form with one unit of fuel. The case $\ev{bound}$ produces $\form{bnd}\,i\,\form{id}$, and $\ev{intoBnd}$ produces $\form{into}\,[\,\form{bnd}\,F\,]$ from the form $F$ of its premise.
\item The case $\ev{trans}$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:combine}, $\ev{pair}$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:pair-apply}, and $\ev{obj}$ is item 4 applied to the morphism.
\item In item 4 a $\ev{bnd}$ template becomes a bound entry by item 1. A composition template $\ev{leTrans}\,m\,p\,q$ becomes a composition entry, because item 4 types the entries of $p$ and of $q$ at a telescope with one inclusion, and such entries are a single typed local entry.
\item For $\ev{member}\,a\,e\,i$, item 5 gives a typed view of $a$, and item 1 gives a typed form of $e$ whose target is an object type. Hence the source of $e$ resolves to an object type, and the view of $a$ through the form of $e$ is a typed view of the target telescope by Lemma~\ref{lem:pair-apply}. Its entry at $i$ is a typed form, an equality of resolutions, or a field of the object at the root, according to the kind of the proposition.
\item The case $\ev{def}$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:resolve}.
\item For an atom that is a variable, the precise view of the stored literal is typed at the literal's telescope. Each definition equality resolves equally by Lemma~\ref{lem:resolve}, and each presence names a field the literal has.
\item A cast atom pushes its view through the form of the cast. Through a bound cast it is the view of the root through the composite of the bound's form and the rest of the cast.
\item Folding and unfolding keep the view, since a typed view of $\tel$ is a typed view of the opened telescope and conversely.
\end{itemize}
The fuel of each case exceeds the largest fuel of its premises by a constant, and monotonicity makes that fuel enough for every premise at once.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Chain of casts]\label{thm:chain}
If $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$ and $\atomj{\Gamma}{a}{S}$ then $\chn{\sigma}{a}{n}{F}$ for some $n$ and $F$ with
\[ \chainj{\Gamma}{\rt{a}}{F}{\Gamma(\rt{a})}{S} . \]
\end{theorem}

The chain theorem types the composite of the casts of an atom from the type of its root to the type of the atom, at the root. The view of an atom and its chain of casts are established by one induction on atom typing. The chain composes the forms of the casts by Lemma~\ref{lem:combine} in the mode at the root, pairs them by Lemma~\ref{lem:pair-apply}, and uses that opening at the root is stable under folding and unfolding.

\begin{corollary}[Shapes of closed inclusions]\label{cor:shapes}
Let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$ and $\lej{\Gamma}{e}{S}{T}$. Then $S$ resolves to $\bot$, or $T$ resolves to $\top$, or $S$ and $T$ resolve equally, or both resolve to function types, or both resolve to object types, or $S$ resolves to an object type one of whose bounds is included in $T$ by a typed form, or $T$ resolves to an object type all of whose propositions are bounds. In particular there is no closed evidence for $\top \le \bot$, none from an object type without bounds into a function type, and none from a function type into an object type with a proposition that is not a bound.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Inversion at the store]\label{cor:inversion}
Let $\storej{\sigma}{\Gamma}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\atomj{\Gamma}{a}{\pit{S}{T}}$, then $\sigma(\rt{a})$ is a lambda, and the chain of casts of $a$ normalizes to $\form{id}$ or to a function form.
\item If $\hasj{\Gamma}{h}{x}{\ell}$, then $\sigma(x)$ is a literal with a field $\ell$.
\item For every binder $x$ and label $\ell$ there is a $W$ with $\Gamma(x) \ni \ell \mathrel{:=} W$ and
\[ \eqj{\Gamma}{\ev{def}\,x\,\ell}{\sel{x}{\ell}}{W} . \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

The first corollary is the counterpart of what invertible typing provides in the DOT proofs, proven once, over stores, with no restriction on recursive members and no semantic model. The second is exactly what progress needs.
