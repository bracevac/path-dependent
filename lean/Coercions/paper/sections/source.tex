Figure~\ref{fig:source-syntax} gives the syntax. The calculus is WadlerFest DOT \cite{amin2016essence} with two presentational choices. Object literals carry no type annotation, since the translation is directed by derivations and definition typing determines the declaration type. And all syntax is intrinsically scoped: a term of signature $s$ mentions only the binders of $s$, and every judgment is indexed by its signature. We write $\weak{T}$ for weakening under a new binder and $\inst{T}{y}$ for instantiating the innermost binder by the variable $y$. Every substitution performed by typing or by the machine is a renaming.

\paragraph{Restrictions.}
The labels of a definition list must be distinct, which \rn{\{\}-I} checks. Nothing else about the WadlerFest calculus is restricted. A recursive type may have any body, including a selection, a function type, $\bot$, or an intersection with such an operand, as in $\dmu{x}{x.A \wedge \dfld{a}{T}}$. Intersections are unrestricted, and the bounds of a declaration are arbitrary types: \rn{Typ} relates $\dtyp{A}{S}{T}$ to other declarations whether or not $S \sub T$. The first version of the development restricted the bodies of recursive types and the operands of intersections to declaration shapes, because the target had no telescope for anything else, and Section~\ref{sec:forced} says what lifting the two restrictions took.

Figure~\ref{fig:source-typing} gives subtyping, typing, and definition typing. The three judgments are mutually inductive, because \rn{Sel-<:} and \rn{<:-Sel} have typing premises and a variable typing may go through subsumption. In the mechanization the three judgments are inductive families in $\mathsf{Type}$, because the translation is a function on derivations.

The rule \rn{\{\}-I} binds the self variable at the recursive type $\dmu{x}{T}$ rather than at the opened body, because in an intrinsically scoped context an entry lives in the signature before its own binder. The two presentations are interderivable through \rn{Rec-I} and \rn{Rec-E}, which is how the published rules are related to these at the end of this section. The context also records the definitions of a literal at its self binder, written $\Gamma, x : \dmu{x}{T} \mathrel{:=} d$. Typing does not distinguish such a binder from an ordinary one. The translation does.

\paragraph{Machine.}
A state $\mst{\sigma}{K}{t}$ is a store of values, a continuation of let frames, and a term, all over one signature. Allocation extends the signature by one binder, and $\weak{K}$ weakens the continuation under it. Figure~\ref{fig:source-machine} gives the steps.

A state is final when the continuation is empty and the term is a value or a variable. On lookup in a definition list the right conjunct shadows the left.

\paragraph{Erasure.}
The runtime is an untyped store machine with the same five rules, whose object literals keep term members only. Erasure drops types and type members and is the identity on signatures. Every source step erases to exactly one runtime step, and every runtime step out of an erased state is the erasure of a source step. These two facts, \lean{erase\_step} and \lean{erase\_reflect}, and the agreement of final states under erasure, \lean{final\_erase} and \lean{final\_reflect}, are the only metatheory the source calculus has.

\paragraph{The published calculus.}
\DOTMNF differs from the calculus of the WadlerFest paper in its presentation of objects and in its semantics. The published rules annotate an object literal with its self type, $\src{\nu(x : T)\,d}$, and type its definitions under the opened assumption $x : T$, which mentions $x$ itself. The published semantics is a reduction relation on terms that retains let-bound values in the term: a value bound by a let stays in place, reduction continues in the body under that binding, and application and selection look their receiver up among the enclosing bindings. A let reduces in its right-hand side and, once that is a value, in its body, and nested lets reassociate, with no priority between reassociation and reduction in the right-hand side. The development states the published rules and the reduction relation as the paper gives them and relates both to \DOTMNF. Every closed derivation of the published rules translates to a \DOTMNF derivation of the term with its annotations erased, where the opened assumption of a literal's self is recovered by \rn{Rec-E} from the recursive binder. The proof assumes nothing about the contexts it passes through, neither inertness nor good bounds nor acyclic definitions. In the other direction, reduction and the store machine agree. A closed term reduces to an answer exactly when the machine runs to a final state whose readback is that answer, where readback plugs the term into the pending let frames and closes it with the store. And a closed term reduces to a stuck term exactly when the machine gets stuck. The only invariant this needs is that the labels of every definition list are distinct. Section~\ref{sec:translation} combines the two directions into the safety of the published calculus. The published grammar also separates type labels from term labels, and the results hold for the sorted fragment as well, which reduction preserves.
